Level 3: The Twist Factory

Welcome to the Twist Factory

You have a pitch, a path and a mover. Right now your game is a race where players roll and move. Now you'll add the parts that make players shout, groan and cheer: special spaces, surprise cards and a way to keep everyone in the game until the end.

Special spaces

A special space makes something happen when you land on it. Here are some types designers use:

  • Shortcut: zoom ahead to another spot.
  • Setback: slide back, lose a turn or drop something.
  • Roll again: take another turn right away.
  • Swap: trade places with another player.
  • Safe zone: nobody can bother you here.
  • Draw a card: pick up a surprise card.
  • Choice space: pick between two things, like "move ahead 3, or take a card."

Make the spaces fit your theme. In a volcano escape game, a setback could be a lava splash and a shortcut could be a zip line. Same idea, much more fun.

Use your dice experiment! If players roll two dice, a space 6, 7 or 8 steps from the start will get landed on a lot, so put a big moment there.

Surprise cards

Cards add surprises that dice can't. A good deck has a mix:

  • Good cards ("A friendly dragon gives you a ride. Move ahead 4.")
  • Bad cards ("Your shoelace snaps. Lose a turn.")
  • Choice cards ("Take a shortcut, but give a treasure to the player on your left.")
  • Everybody cards ("Earthquake! Everyone moves back 2.")

Keep each card short enough to read in one breath. Players want to get back to the game!

Keep everyone in the race

Here's a problem every designer faces. If one player gets way ahead early, everyone else starts to feel like there's no point. Game designers who study why players quit say it often happens when players feel the game won't give them a chance, no matter how well they play.

Designer's Secret: good games keep everyone in the race, with surprises and a way back.

Designers call this catch-up. Some ways to do it:

  • The player in last place rolls an extra die.
  • The leader can't use shortcuts.
  • Some cards help whoever is behind.
  • A big surprise late in the game can change everything.

Don't overload your players

Choices make games fun, but too many at once can freeze a player up. Designers call it analysis paralysis: the player has so many options that they can't decide, and everyone else is stuck waiting.

The fix is to give players a few clear choices at a time, usually two or three. "Left or right?" is exciting. A list of fifteen things to pick from is homework.

Design Quest: The Twist Factory

Part 1: Design 6 to 10 kinds of spaces for your game, including at least one choice space. Mark where they go on your path.

Part 2: Write 10 surprise cards, with a mix of good, bad and choice cards.

Part 3: Add one catch-up rule so players who fall behind still have a chance.
