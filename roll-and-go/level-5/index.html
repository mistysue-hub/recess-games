Level 5: The Hall of Games

The final level

You invented a game, built it and survived the playtest. One last level stands between you and the title of Master Designer. Today you'll make your game ready for the world: a rulebook anyone could follow and a box cover that makes people want to play.

Why do fans come back?

Some games get played once and forgotten. Others, like Snakes and Ladders, get played for more than a hundred years. What's the difference?

Psychologists at the University of Rochester studied why people keep coming back to games. They found three feelings that mattered most:

  • Choice: feeling like you're in control of what you do
  • Growing skill: feeling like you're getting better and can succeed
  • Good company: feeling connected to the people you play with

Players who felt those things enjoyed games more and were more likely to want to play again.

Designer's Secret: fans come back to games that give them choice, growing skill and good company.

And because roll-and-go games have luck, no two games are ever the same. Every game makes a new story to talk about afterward.

Write a rulebook

A rulebook has one job: let someone who has never seen your game play it without you there to explain. Good rulebooks go in this order:

  1. The goal: one sentence on how to win.
  2. What's in the box: the board, pieces, dice and cards.
  3. Setup: where everything goes before you start.
  4. Taking a turn: what a player does, step by step.
  5. Special spaces and cards: what each one does.
  6. Winning: the exact moment the game ends.

Use short sentences. Add a small drawing if a rule is tricky. Then hand your rulebook to someone and watch them try to follow it. Every time they ask you a question, that's a spot to fix in the rules.

Design your box cover

The cover is the first thing a player sees, so it has to show what kind of fun is inside. A great cover has:

  • the title, big and easy to read
  • a picture of the most exciting moment in your game
  • how many players can play
  • the ages it's good for
  • about how long one game takes

Think about the kinds of fun you picked back in Level 1. If your game is about make-believe and a big story moment, your cover should feel like an adventure.
