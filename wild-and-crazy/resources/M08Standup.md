# Set Card 8: Your Set

## Walking away at the top

In 1981 Steve Martin was selling out arenas, one of the biggest draws stand-up had ever seen, and he stopped for good. He has said his act was built on one idea, and once everyone knew the idea there was nothing left to develop. He didn't want to perform a copy of himself. So he quit, and made movies.

It looked like an ending. It was the start of four more careers: actor, writer, banjo player with real Grammys, and lately a TV detective in Only Murders in the Building, alongside his best friend.

## The friend

Steve met Martin Short making Three Amigos in 1985. On stage they roast each other constantly, and the audience howls, because every insult is obviously a love letter. There's a kind of comedy that only works between people who trust each other. It might be the best kind. (Set Card 3 said jokes aimed at a character you built last longer than jokes aimed at someone who can't answer back. A friend who roasts you right back is a third case: you're both in on it.)

## What this was about

No punchlines. Empty rooms. The confident fool. Holding the tension. High and low. Every word. The notebook. Underneath all of it: comedy isn't a gift that lands on you. It's a craft, built by someone who kept showing up, kept cutting, kept noticing, and stayed kind about who the joke is on.

You've written a no-punchline joke, an opener, a fool, a tension bit, a juxtaposition, a cut-down joke, and a mini set. That's a set, hiding in pieces.
