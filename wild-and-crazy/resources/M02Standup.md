# Set Card 2: The Ten Years Nobody Came

## A kid on a bike

Before anyone had heard of him, Steve Martin rode his bike to Disneyland to sell guidebooks. As a teenager he worked the counter at the park's magic shop, doing the same tricks a hundred times a day for strangers who mostly wandered off. Across town at Knott's Berry Farm he did a tiny stage show, four times a day, for whoever sat down.

That was his comedy school: not a class, a counter. Thousands of tiny audiences, most of them unimpressed.

## The years that weren't a montage

He got a good job writing jokes for TV, won an award for it, and quit to perform his own act in clubs. His agents told him not to. What followed was years of small rooms, late slots, and audiences that didn't laugh, followed by a drive to the next town to do it again.

What he did with those rooms: he kept notes. After every show he wrote down what landed and what didn't, and changed one small thing. He treated each audience as information, not a verdict.

Asked later how to make it, he gave the same answer every time: be so good they can't ignore you. (His words, on the Charlie Rose show, 2007.)

## Tonight's clip: the magic act

This is Steve on his first TV job, doing a magic act where the tricks are the joke. Watch what he does when one "fails." The failure is the act.
