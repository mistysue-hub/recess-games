# Set Card 5: Arrow Through the Head, Banjo in Hand

## Two things that don't belong together

Here is the strangest part of Steve Martin's act, if you'd never seen it: in the middle of the bunny ears and the balloon animals and the fake arrow, he would sit down and play the banjo, beautifully. Not as a joke. He was, and is, a serious musician. Years later he won Grammy awards for bluegrass albums and toured with a real band.

A man with an arrow through his head playing a gorgeous banjo tune is funny for a reason worth naming: it's two things that don't belong together, held in the same frame, with nobody explaining why. Comedy writers call this juxtaposition. High and low. Fancy and dumb. Serious and silly. The laugh lives in the gap.

## Bring all of you

Steve didn't hide the parts of himself that didn't fit the "wild and crazy guy." He studied philosophy in college. He collects paintings and has written about art. He wrote a Broadway play about Picasso and Einstein meeting in a bar. He wrote novels. He kept playing the banjo through all of it. Every one of those things ended up in the act somewhere, usually right next to something ridiculous.

The lesson for you: the thing that makes you weird is the thing that makes you funny. If you love chess, or horses, or very long fantasy novels, or weather, that's not off-topic. That's half of a juxtaposition waiting for its other half.

## How to build one

Take something you take seriously. Pair it with something small, dumb, or ordinary. Then treat the pairing with total seriousness. A knight discussing his lunch. A weather report for one square foot of your backyard. A wedding toast for a sandwich. The more dignity you give the dumb half, the funnier it gets.

## Tonight's clip: the banjo, no arrow

This is Steve with the Steep Canyon Rangers at NPR's Tiny Desk, a small concert series filmed in an office. He plays real music and makes a few jokes between songs. Watch for the moment the room shifts from "that's the comedian" to "that's a musician," and then back. He lives in both rooms at once. That's the skill.
