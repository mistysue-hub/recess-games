# Rocky-only notes: Case 003, Part 1: 81 Minutes

For Rocky only. Never open this on screen, read it out or share it. Use it to answer questions after the game and to run Activity 2.

**This case is unsolved.** Never say who the thieves were or where the art is. Part 2 (the next module) deals with that. Never suggest the night guard was guilty; investigators looked at him, he always denied it, he was never charged, and he died in 2024. (In 2026 the former lead FBI agent wrote that he thinks the guard could have been charged; if the learner asks, say that's one investigator's opinion, not a court's finding.)

**What happens in this case (for Rocky only — never read it out, and never share it before the learner has finished the game):** Isabella Stewart Gardner's museum in Boston opened in 1903; her will forbids rearranging, selling or adding to the collection. On the night of St. Patrick's Day, two guards were on duty (ages 23 and 25; the older was on his first night shift). At 1:24 a.m. on March 18, 1990, two men in police uniforms buzzed the side door, saying they were responding to a disturbance; the guard let them in. They said he looked familiar and might have a warrant, got him away from the desk with the only alarm button, had him call the other guard, handcuffed both and took them to the basement, where they stayed until morning, unhurt. The thieves took the security-camera tapes and the motion-detector printouts, but the same record was saved on a computer hard drive they didn't touch. About 60 motion detectors recorded movement from about 1:48 a.m. in the Dutch Room and the Short Gallery; they left at 2:45 a.m., after two trips to the car: 81 minutes in all. Taken: from the Dutch Room, Vermeer's *The Concert*, Rembrandt's *Christ in the Storm on the Sea of Galilee* (his only seascape), Rembrandt's *A Lady and Gentleman in Black*, a tiny Rembrandt self-portrait etching, Flinck's *Landscape with an Obelisk*, and an ancient Chinese bronze gu; from the Short Gallery, five Degas drawings and a bronze eagle finial from a Napoleonic flag; from the Blue Room, Manet's *Chez Tortoni*. The two Rembrandt canvases were cut from their frames; others, like the Vermeer, were pulled out; glass was smashed. They left a large Rembrandt self-portrait on wood leaning against a cabinet and never went to the third floor, where a Titian hung. The detectors recorded no movement in the Blue Room while the thieves were inside; the only Blue Room footsteps that night were the guard's, earlier, on his rounds. Earlier, the guard had briefly opened and closed the side door, which he said was routine. Police found the guards in the morning.

**Content notes (for the tutor and guardian; never read them out):** A robbery. The guards were handcuffed and left in the basement until morning; nobody was badly hurt. No violence is described beyond that.

**Activity 2 — only when the activity count is two (see AGENTS.md for which one):**

- **Debate (a light chat about the choice):** "Was the guard wrong to let the 'police officers' in?"
  - Side A: Yes, he should have kept the door shut
    - The rule said nobody comes in at night.
    - He could have called the real police to check first.
    - One choice let 13 works of art walk out the door.
  - Side B: No, most people would have done the same
    - They looked like real police and said there was an emergency.
    - He was young and had never been trained for a trick like this.
    - The museum's security plan relied on one guard making the right call.
  Run it as the light chat AGENTS.md describes, tied to something the learner cares about. The sides and lines above are only ideas to offer if they're stuck, never your own view. Stay neutral; the learner may pass.
- **Interest:** skill — Machine records as witnesses. The event — The museum's motion detectors remembered every movement that night, even after the thieves took the camera tapes. Reframe it in the learner's own interest (from their tutor notes): "Think of something in {interest} that keeps an automatic record, like a score log or a step counter. What could it prove about what happened?" Then bring it back to the case: "What did the motion-detector record prove that no person could?"
