# CASE FILE 002 — PART 1

**Case name:** Spoons, Soap and Fake Heads
**Place:** Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary, San Francisco Bay, California
**Date of crime:** The night of Monday, June 11, 1962
**Missing:** Frank Morris · John Anglin · Clarence Anglin
**Status:** UNSOLVED

---

## Welcome back, Detective

Your last case started with a man who disappeared into the sky. This one starts with three men who disappeared from inside a locked cell. There was no witness to the escape itself. Almost everything we know comes from the things they left behind. Read this file the way investigators read those cells: one clue at a time, in order.

---

## Part 1: The Rock

Alcatraz is a small, rocky island in the middle of San Francisco Bay. From 1934 to 1963 it was a federal prison, and it held men who had escaped from other prisons or caused trouble in them. People called it **"The Rock."**

It was built so that nobody could get out. Guards counted the prisoners many times every day and checked the cells through the night. Gun towers and searchlights watched the island. And even if a prisoner got outside, the bay was cold, usually in the 50s °F, with strong currents that rush in and out under the Golden Gate Bridge.

No prisoner was known to have escaped from Alcatraz and made it to freedom.

---

## Part 2: Four Men, One Plan

**EXHIBIT A: The four prisoners**

- **Frank Morris**, serving time for robbery. He was known for being very smart and very patient. Many investigators think he was the planner.
- **John Anglin** and **Clarence Anglin**, brothers from a big family in the South, serving time for robbing a bank in Alabama.
- **Allen West**, serving time for car theft.

Their cells were close together in the same cellblock. Late in 1961, they began to plan.

---

## Part 3: Months of Quiet Work

At the back of every cell, under the sink, was a small air vent covered by a metal grille. The concrete around it was old, and the salty sea air had been weakening it for years.

The men started to dig.

**EXHIBIT B: The tools**

- metal spoons, taken from the dining hall
- old saw blades, found on the prison grounds
- a homemade drill, built from the motor of a broken vacuum cleaner

Digging makes noise. So every day during "music hour," when prisoners were allowed to play instruments, Frank Morris played his accordion. Its loud music helped cover the scraping.

At night, they hid the growing holes behind fake vent covers made from cardboard, painted to look like metal. A guard glancing into a cell saw nothing unusual.

It took months.

---

## Part 4: The Secret Workshop

Behind the cells ran a narrow, dark **utility corridor**, full of pipes and wires. Once the vents were wide enough, the men could crawl through into it at night.

They climbed the pipes to the very top of the cellblock, where nobody went. There, hidden from the guards, they built a **secret workshop**. They hung blankets to hide what they were doing.

---

## Part 5: The Raft

Getting out of the building was only half the problem. They still had to cross the bay.

**EXHIBIT C: The raft and life vests**

- more than **50 raincoats**, taken from around the prison
- the raincoats' rubbery cloth, stitched together by hand into **life vests** and a raft about **6 feet wide and 14 feet long**
- seams sealed with liquid plastic from the prison shops, set with heat from the hot steam pipes
- paddles made from scraps of wood
- an accordion-style instrument turned into a **pump** for blowing the raft full of air

They got some of their ideas from a popular science and how-to magazine.

---

## Part 6: The Heads

The guards checked the cells through the night. If a bed was empty, the alarm would sound within minutes. So the men needed something in their beds that looked like a sleeping prisoner.

**EXHIBIT D: The dummy heads**

Each man made a fake head from **soap, toothpaste, concrete dust and toilet paper**. They painted the faces with paint from the maintenance shop. Then they added **real human hair**, gathered from the floor of the prison barbershop.

In the dim light of a cell at night, tucked under a blanket, a fake head looked like a man asleep.

---

## Part 7: The Night of June 11

At **9:30 p.m.**, the lights went out.

Frank Morris, John Anglin and Clarence Anglin put their dummy heads in their beds, pulled the blankets up, and squeezed out through the holes behind their sinks. They climbed to the workshop, gathered the raft and vests, and pushed up through a ventilation shaft onto the roof.

From the roof, they climbed down about **50 feet** by sliding down a pipe on the outside wall. They crossed the prison yard in the dark, climbed **two tall fences topped with barbed wire**, and reached the shore on the northeast side of the island, near the power plant. That spot was hidden from the searchlights and gun towers.

There, they used the pump to fill the raft with air, and pushed off into the black water.

**Allen West never made it.** He had used cement to hold his loose vent cover in place. The cement hardened, and his vent jammed. By the time he finally broke through, the others were gone.

---

## Part 8: The Morning Count

All night, guards walked past the three cells. Each time, they saw what looked like a sleeping man.

At about **7:00 a.m.** on June 12, a guard reached into Clarence Anglin's cell to wake him. The head rolled off the pillow and fell onto the floor.

It was made of soap.

The alarm sounded across the island. Within minutes, guards found two more fake heads, three holes behind three sinks, and a fourth prisoner, Allen West, who knew the whole plan.

---

## Detective's Notes

- No one saw the escape. The story is pieced together from the evidence: the holes, the tools, the workshop, the heads.
- Every step needed the step before it. The roof was useless until the vents were open.
- Allen West is the only witness. He knows the plan, but not the ending.

**NIGHT OF JUNE 11–12, 1962**

`9:30 p.m.` Lights out → out through the vents → up to the roof → down the pipe, over the fences → into the water → `7:00 a.m.` a head hits the floor

Where did they go? That's Part 2.

**END OF FILE**
