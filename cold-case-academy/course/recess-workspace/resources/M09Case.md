# CASE FILE 005

**Case name:** Seven Minutes at the Louvre
**Place:** The Apollo Gallery, Louvre Museum, Paris, France
**Date of crime:** Sunday, October 19, 2025, about 9:30 a.m.
**Missing:** 8 pieces of the French Crown Jewels
**Status:** SUSPECTS CHARGED · JEWELS STILL MISSING

---

## Welcome back, Detective

Remember your training case? In 1911, the *Mona Lisa* was stolen from the Louvre, and it came home two years later. This case happened in the same museum, 114 years later. It's the newest case in the academy, and the investigation is still going on as you read this.

In your other cases, nobody was ever caught. This time, the police found the people they believe did it. And yet the most important question is still open. Your job: decide what it really means for a case to be **solved**.

---

## Part 1: A Room Full of Crowns

The Louvre is the most visited museum in the world. Inside it is the **Apollo Gallery**, a long, golden hall with a painted ceiling. In glass cases along its walls were the **French Crown Jewels**: crowns, tiaras, necklaces and brooches that once belonged to French kings, queens and empresses.

Some pieces were covered in thousands of diamonds. Many had been made for Napoleon's family about 200 years ago.

---

## Part 2: The Workers Who Weren't

On a Sunday morning in October, the museum had been open for about half an hour. Visitors were already inside.

Out on the street along the river, a truck parked below the museum wall. On its back was a **furniture lift**: a long ladder with a metal basket on top, the kind movers in Paris use to lift sofas up to apartment windows. Men in bright yellow and orange safety vests stood beside it. To anyone walking by, they looked like ordinary workers.

At about **9:30 a.m.**, two of the men rode the basket up to a balcony on the side of the museum. The window there opens straight into the Apollo Gallery.

---

## Part 3: Inside

The two men cut through the window with a power tool called a **disc cutter**. Alarms went off.

Inside, they cut open two glass display cases and grabbed the jewels. Museum staff called the police, and guards moved visitors out of the way while the thieves threatened them with their tools.

Then the two men climbed back out the window, rode the basket down, and jumped onto **two scooters** waiting with two more members of the team. They tried to set the lift truck on fire to destroy the evidence, but were stopped before it could burn.

Security camera times show the thieves were inside the gallery for **about four minutes**. France's interior minister said the whole theft, start to finish, took about **seven**.

---

## Part 4: What They Took, and What They Dropped

**EXHIBIT A: The 8 missing pieces**

- A sapphire **tiara**, a sapphire **necklace** and one sapphire **earring** worn by two queens, Marie-Amélie of France and Hortense of Holland
- An emerald **necklace** and a pair of emerald **earrings** that Napoleon gave his wife, Empress Marie-Louise
- A pearl-and-diamond **tiara** belonging to Empress Eugénie
- A large diamond **bow brooch** belonging to Empress Eugénie
- A diamond brooch called a **reliquary brooch**

The museum's experts valued them at about **88 million euros**, more than $100 million. But historians say no price can really be put on them.

**EXHIBIT B: The crown on the ground**

The thieves also grabbed Empress Eugénie's **crown**, covered with more than 1,300 diamonds and decorated with eight golden eagles. During the escape, they dropped it. It was found on the ground near the museum, bent and broken, but almost all its diamonds were still there. It's being repaired.

**EXHIBIT C: What they didn't take**

In the same gallery was the **Regent Diamond**, one of the most valuable single diamonds in the world. The thieves didn't touch it.

---

## Part 5: The Trail

The thieves left a trail behind them:

- a helmet, gloves, a walkie-talkie and a blanket
- power tools, a blowtorch and a can of gasoline
- the lift truck itself, which never burned

Forensic scientists collected more than 150 samples from the evidence, looking for **DNA**, the tiny traces of skin and hair every person leaves behind. DNA showed up on the scooter, on a display case, and in the basket of the lift.

**EXHIBIT D: The arrests**

- **October 25, 2025:** Six days after the theft, police arrested two men. One was caught at the airport about to board a plane leaving France. Both partly admitted taking part.
- **October 29:** Police arrested more people. A few days later, a third man was charged. His DNA had been found in the lift basket.
- **November 25:** Police arrested a fourth man, and three days later he was charged. Investigators believe he was the last member of the team of four.

A woman was also charged with helping. Several other people were questioned and let go.

Being **charged** means a court will decide. It doesn't mean guilty yet. In France, the case will go to trial, but no trial had started as of October 2026.

---

## Part 6: Where Are the Jewels?

None of the eight pieces has been found.

In 2026, two of the men told investigators that someone else hired them for the job. One of them said he handed the jewels to that man in a parking garage near Paris, soon after the theft. Neither will say who he is, and investigators still aren't sure he exists. Camera footage from that garage shows two of the suspects there with the jewels less than an hour after the theft. Nobody knows who planned the theft or who has the jewels now.

In June 2026, French newspapers reported a new lead: photos of the Apollo Gallery had turned up on the phones of other suspects in a Belgian investigation. Police looked into whether people there helped hide or sell the jewels. Officials haven't said what the lead turned up.

The jewels are so famous that nobody could sell them as they are. Some experts fear the thieves have already taken out the stones and melted down the gold, so the pieces could never be recognized again. Others think they may still be hidden, waiting.

---

## Part 7: After the Theft

The Louvre was shocked. An inquiry found that only one of the two cameras near the break-in spot was working, and that older safety warnings had never been fully fixed. In February 2026, the head of the museum stepped down.

The Apollo Gallery reopened in July 2026, with new cameras and alarms, but with no jewels at all. The display cases were taken out. The jewels that weren't stolen were moved somewhere safer, and the museum plans to show them again in a new room with no windows.

---

## Detective's Notes

- **How** it was done is known in great detail. **Who** did the stealing has been charged. **Who** planned it, and **where** the jewels are, nobody knows.
- DNA can point to a person. It can't tell you where the loot went.
- The *Mona Lisa* came back after two years. Not every treasure does.
