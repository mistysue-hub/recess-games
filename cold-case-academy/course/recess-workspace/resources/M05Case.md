# CASE FILE 002 — PART 2

**Case name:** Did They Make It?
**Place:** San Francisco Bay, and anywhere three men could hide
**Dates:** June 1962 to today
**Missing:** Frank Morris · John Anglin · Clarence Anglin
**Status:** UNSOLVED

---

## Welcome back, Detective

In Part 1, three men slipped out of Alcatraz on a raft made of raincoats. In this file, you'll follow the hunt for them, which has lasted more than 60 years. Some clues in this file are **facts** that were tested. Some are **claims**: things people said, which nobody has been able to test. Keep track of which is which.

---

## Part 1: The Search

By the morning of June 12, 1962, boats, planes and police were searching the bay. The FBI joined the hunt. Over the next ten days, searchers found:

**EXHIBIT E: What the bay gave back**

- **A paddle**, floating about 200 yards off the southern shore of **Angel Island**, a big island north of Alcatraz.
- **A packet wrapped in plastic**, holding the names, addresses and photos of the Anglin brothers' friends and relatives.
- **A homemade life vest**, washed up on a beach nearly three miles outside the Golden Gate Bridge.
- **Another life vest**, floating just off Alcatraz, its strings still knotted at the back.

No raft was confirmed. No bodies were ever found.

---

## Part 2: The Witness Who Stayed Behind

Allen West, the fourth man, told investigators everything he knew. He said the plan was to paddle to Angel Island, cross to the mainland, steal clothes and a car, and disappear.

But West wasn't there when the others pushed off. He knew the plan, not what happened next.

---

## Part 3: The Water

The bay is cold, usually in the 50s °F. Its tides rush in and out under the Golden Gate Bridge, and they can pull a small boat in the wrong direction fast.

Before 1962, more than 30 men had tried to escape Alcatraz. Most were caught. Some drowned. Some were never found, and were believed drowned.

Then, six months after this escape, in December 1962, a prisoner named **John Paul Scott** got out and swam for it, using rubber gloves he'd blown up like balloons to help him float. He made it all the way to the shore near the Golden Gate Bridge. He was found there, exhausted and dangerously cold, and taken straight back.

So a person *could* reach the shore. But it nearly killed him.

---

## Part 4: The FBI's Answer

The FBI investigated for 17 years. On **December 31, 1979**, it closed its case. Its conclusion: the three men **most likely drowned**.

The FBI's reasons:

- The water was cold and the currents strong.
- The FBI found no reports of stolen clothes or cars that fit the plan.
- The men had no money, and their families weren't wealthy enough to hide them.
- No believable sighting had ever turned up.

But the case didn't close for everyone. The **U.S. Marshals**, the agency that hunts for escaped prisoners, took it over and kept the three men on its wanted list.

---

## Part 5: Clues That They Made It

Over the years, new claims appeared.

**EXHIBIT F: The Christmas cards.** Members of the Anglin family say the brothers' mother received Christmas cards signed by John and Clarence for a few years after the escape. The family believed the handwriting was theirs. But nobody could prove when the cards were written.

**EXHIBIT G: The photo from Brazil.** A family friend later showed a photo he said he took in Brazil in 1975. It shows two men who look like the Anglin brothers. A forensic expert thought it was "more than likely" them. But the photo is old and blurry, and both men are wearing sunglasses.

**EXHIBIT H: The letter.** In 2013, a letter arrived at a San Francisco police station. The writer claimed to be John Anglin. "We all made it that night but barely," it said. It claimed that Frank Morris and Clarence Anglin had since died, and that the writer was old and sick. The FBI tested the letter for fingerprints, DNA and handwriting. The results were **inconclusive**: they couldn't prove it was real, and they couldn't prove it was fake.

**EXHIBIT I: The experiments.**

- In 2003, the TV show *MythBusters* built a raft from raincoats, using the same kinds of materials, and paddled it to land near the north end of the Golden Gate Bridge.
- In 2014, scientists at Delft University in the Netherlands built a computer model of the bay's tides. They found that if the men launched at around **11:30 p.m.**, the current could have carried them to land near **Horseshoe Bay**, just inside the Golden Gate Bridge. Leave at the wrong time, and the water could carry them somewhere else entirely. Timing was everything.
- In 2025, engineer and YouTuber Mark Rober and a team built their own raincoat raft and crossed the bay during the right tide.

These experiments show the escape was **possible**. They don't show that it **happened**.

---

## Part 6: Clues That They Didn't

- Cold water, darkness, and a homemade raft nobody had ever tested.
- They didn't know the tides the way the scientists do now. The timing had to be just right.
- The paddle and the life vests were found floating in the bay.
- No confirmed sighting of any of the three men, ever.
- To start new lives, they would have needed money, papers and help.

---

## Part 7: The Case Today

In 2022, the U.S. Marshals released computer-made pictures showing what the three men might look like as very old men. A marshal on the case once explained the rule: the Marshals keep hunting a fugitive until he is caught, proven dead, or would be about 99 years old.

Frank Morris was born in 1926, so he would now be about 100. The Anglin brothers would be in their 90s.

When this file was written, in October 2026, we couldn't find any announcement that the case had closed.

---

## Detective's Notes

- **Possible** is not the same as **proven**.
- A test you can repeat (like a raft experiment) is a different kind of evidence than a claim you can't check (like a photo or a letter).
- When the evidence points both ways, "we can't tell yet" is an honest verdict.
