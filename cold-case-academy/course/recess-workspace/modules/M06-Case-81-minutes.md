# Case 003, Part 1: 81 Minutes

**Start here:** (1) Say the hook vote below, word for word, before opening anything. (2) Open `resources/M06Case.md` as a manila case-file folder and let the learner read it whole. (3) When they say they're done, check their reading time against the finished-too-fast check (Estimated time) and push back once if it's too short; then look back at the vote in one line, then ask the two talk-back questions, one at a time. (4) Open the case game, *The Sensor Log*, at https://mistysue-hub.github.io/recess-games/cold-case-academy/sensor-log/ and let the learner play it to the "Case closed" screen.

## Summary

The third real case. Early on March 18, 1990, two men dressed as Boston police talked their way into the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum, tied up the two night guards, and spent 81 minutes taking 13 works of art, including a Vermeer and three Rembrandts. The museum's motion detectors recorded where they went, except in the one room where the record makes no sense. This part rebuilds the night and finds the fact that doesn't fit.

An art theft; the guards were tied up but not badly hurt.

## Estimated time

Estimated time: 30 minutes

Reading is about 980 words (about 7 minutes at 150 words a minute). The hook and talk-back add about 5 minutes, and the game takes about 15. A second activity adds about 5–10 more when the activity count is two. A learner can pause at any point.

**Finished-too-fast check:** under 5 minutes of reading. That's 75% of the reading time.

## Learning targets

After this module the learner can:

- explain how the motion-detector record lets investigators rebuild a night nobody else saw
- name what was taken and what was strangely left behind
- explain why the silent Blue Room is the fact that doesn't fit

## Tutor guidance

Read AGENTS.md first; it outranks this section and sets the fixed session flow for every module in this course.

**This case is unsolved.** Never say who the thieves were or where the art is. Part 2 (the next module) deals with that. Never suggest the night guard was guilty; investigators looked at him, he always denied it, he was never charged, and he died in 2024. (In 2026 the former lead FBI agent wrote that he thinks the guard could have been charged; if the learner asks, say that's one investigator's opinion, not a court's finding.)

**What happens in this case (for Rocky only — never read it out, and never share it before the learner has read the file):** Isabella Stewart Gardner's museum in Boston opened in 1903; her will forbids rearranging, selling or adding to the collection. On the night of St. Patrick's Day, two guards were on duty (ages 23 and 25; the older was on his first night shift). At 1:24 a.m. on March 18, 1990, two men in police uniforms buzzed the side door, saying they were responding to a disturbance; the guard let them in. They said he looked familiar and might have a warrant, got him away from the desk with the only alarm button, had him call the other guard, handcuffed both and took them to the basement, where they stayed until morning, unhurt. The thieves took the security-camera tapes and the motion-detector printouts, but the same record was saved on a computer hard drive they didn't touch. About 60 motion detectors recorded movement from about 1:48 a.m. in the Dutch Room and the Short Gallery; they left at 2:45 a.m., after two trips to the car: 81 minutes in all. Taken: from the Dutch Room, Vermeer's *The Concert*, Rembrandt's *Christ in the Storm on the Sea of Galilee* (his only seascape), Rembrandt's *A Lady and Gentleman in Black*, a tiny Rembrandt self-portrait etching, Flinck's *Landscape with an Obelisk*, and an ancient Chinese bronze gu; from the Short Gallery, five Degas drawings and a bronze eagle finial from a Napoleonic flag; from the Blue Room, Manet's *Chez Tortoni*. The two Rembrandt canvases were cut from their frames; others, like the Vermeer, were pulled out; glass was smashed. They left a large Rembrandt self-portrait on wood leaning against a cabinet and never went to the third floor, where a Titian hung. The detectors recorded no movement in the Blue Room while the thieves were inside; the only Blue Room footsteps that night were the guard's, earlier, on his rounds. Earlier, the guard had briefly opened and closed the side door, which he said was routine. Police found the guards in the morning.

**Content notes (for the tutor and guardian; never read them out):** A robbery. The guards were handcuffed and left in the basement until morning; nobody was badly hurt. No violence is described beyond that.

**Hook — present word for word, exactly as below, before anything else:**

> Quick vote. Two thieves spent 81 minutes inside a famous Boston museum. How do you think they got in?
>
> A) They broke in through a window
> B) They dressed as police officers, and a guard let them in
> C) They hid inside until the museum closed

If the learner answers in their own words instead of A, B, or C, record their words as given and go straight to the reading. Do not react to the vote either way.

**Reading — open `resources/M06Case.md` whole, exactly as written.** Open the reading as a manila case-file folder (cream/manila background, typewriter-style monospace headings, a red "CASE FILE" stamp near the top), with every word exactly as in the file. Do not summarise it, condense it, or narrate it from memory. Tell the learner, before they start, that they can say if they need to pause and come back later, and to say when they're done with the file. Ask no questions while they're reading.

**Finished too fast? — check before the look-back:** when the learner says they're done, add up their reading time on this case (since the reading opened, plus any earlier sessions on this module in `state/sessions.jsonl`). If it's under 5 minutes, push back once, kindly: say this case file usually takes about 7 minutes to read, and ask them to go back and finish it — they can pause and come back later if they're tired. Don't reveal the vote's answer or ask about the case yet. If they say they really did read it all, take their word and go on.

**Look back at the vote — one line, once the learner has finished reading:** remind them what they voted and say in one sentence whether the case file bore it out (the answer is B). No lecture.

**Talk-back — at most two questions, one at a time (skip entirely if the tutor turned talk-back off):**

1. How do investigators know which rooms the thieves went into?
   *(What a good answer mentions: the motion detectors recorded every movement with the time, and the thieves missed the copy saved on the computer.)*
2. What's strange about the Blue Room?
   *(What a good answer mentions: a painting was taken from it, but the detectors recorded no movement there while the thieves were inside; the only footsteps were the guard's, earlier.)*

Keep it light and about what happened. Never write a card, header, or label anywhere that answers a talk-back question.

**Activity 1 — the game, *The Sensor Log*:** open https://mistysue-hub.github.io/recess-games/cold-case-academy/sensor-log/ (a Twine detective game on an outside webpage; you can't see inside it). Tell the learner once before they start:
- they're a Boston detective arriving at the museum on Sunday morning, with 7 leads to spend;
- a new place costs one lead, and going back to a place they've visited is free;
- interviews allow only two questions;
- the sensor board (free) is where they mark which rooms the detectors say the thieves were in;
- the deduction board (free) joins two clues into a new idea;
- at the end they report how long the thieves were inside, which rooms they visited, and what doesn't fit;
- what they collect is saved to an evidence locker for Part 2 (on the same device).

Don't tell them which rooms the detectors recorded or what doesn't fit. If they're stuck, you may ask, "Where would a record of the night be kept?" or "Was anything taken from a room nobody walked through?" The game ends on a green "Case closed" stamp. Then ask, one at a time: how long the thieves were inside, what didn't fit, and their detective rating (stars out of 3). The game's own last screens explain what investigators know; don't repeat them, but answer questions about them from the Rocky-only notes above.

**Activity 2 — only when the activity count is two (see AGENTS.md for which one):**

- **Debate (a light chat about the choice):** "Was the guard wrong to let the 'police officers' in?"
  - Side A: Yes, he should have kept the door shut
    - The rule said nobody comes in at night.
    - He could have called the real police to check first.
    - One choice let 13 works of art walk out the door.
  - Side B: No, most people would have done the same
    - They looked like real police and said there was an emergency.
    - He was young and had never been trained for a trick like this.
    - The museum's security plan relied on one guard making the right call.
  Run it as the light chat AGENTS.md describes, tied to something the learner cares about. The sides and lines above are only ideas to offer if they're stuck, never your own view. Stay neutral; the learner may pass.
- **Interest:** skill — Machine records as witnesses. The event — The museum's motion detectors remembered every movement that night, even after the thieves took the camera tapes. Reframe it in the learner's own interest (from their tutor notes): "Think of something in {interest} that keeps an automatic record, like a score log or a step counter. What could it prove about what happened?" Then bring it back to the case: "What did the motion-detector record prove that no person could?"

**Book suggestion — always offer, never require:** once the activities are done, suggest *Unsolved Case Files: The 500 Million Dollar Heist* by Tom Sullivan, a graphic novel that tells the Gardner theft minute by minute, from the same series as the D.B. Cooper and Alcatraz books. Say it's just for fun if they want more; don't assign it or check on it later.

When the activity (or both activities) is finished, the book has been suggested, and the learner confirms they're done, the module is complete.
