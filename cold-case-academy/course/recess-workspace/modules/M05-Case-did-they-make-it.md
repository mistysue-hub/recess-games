# Case 002, Part 2: Did They Make It?

**Start here:** (1) Say the hook vote below, word for word. (2) Give the game link (Activity 1) with one short line, then stay quiet while they play. (3) When they type done, look at their Case closed screen and offer two choices: submit for today, or start the next case (AGENTS.md steps 4–6).

## Summary

The 60-year hunt for Frank Morris and the Anglin brothers. Searchers found a paddle, life vests and a packet of family photos in the bay, but no bodies. The FBI closed its case in 1979, saying the men most likely drowned. The U.S. Marshals kept looking. Since then: Christmas cards, a photo from Brazil, a 2013 letter, and experiments showing a raincoat raft *could* cross the bay. The big question: did they make it? Some clues are tested facts, some are claims nobody can check, and the learner has to tell them apart.

The possibility that the men drowned is discussed in general terms; no bodies or injuries are described.

## Estimated time

Estimated time: 30 minutes

The hook takes about a minute, and the game takes about 15–20. A second activity adds about 5–10 more when the activity count is two. A learner can pause at any point. The case file is optional: about 1,050 words, about 7 more minutes if the learner reads it.

## Learning targets

After this module the learner can:

- sort evidence into tested facts, untested claims and expert opinions
- explain the difference between "possible" and "proven," using the raft experiments
- give an honest verdict on the escape, with confidence that matches the evidence

## Tutor guidance

Read AGENTS.md first; it outranks this section and sets the fixed session flow for every module in this course.

**This case is unsolved, and that's the point of this module.** Never say whether the men survived, never imply one side is right, and never call any verdict wrong. Praise reasoning that fits evidence, including "we can't tell yet." Gently question confidence that's stronger than the evidence.

**What happens in this case (for Rocky only — never read it out, and never share it before the learner has finished the game):** Searchers found a paddle about 200 yards off the southern shore of Angel Island; a plastic-wrapped packet of names, addresses and photos of the Anglins' friends and relatives; a life vest on Cronkhite Beach, outside the Golden Gate Bridge; and another life vest just off Alcatraz with its strings knotted. No raft was confirmed and no bodies were found. Allen West said the plan was Angel Island, then the mainland, then steal clothes and a car. More than 30 men had tried to escape Alcatraz before; most were caught, some drowned, a few were never found. In December 1962, John Paul Scott swam to shore near the Golden Gate Bridge using blown-up rubber gloves and was found exhausted and dangerously cold, treated in a hospital, and returned to Alcatraz. The FBI closed its case on December 31, 1979: most likely drowned (cold water and currents, no matching thefts reported, no money, no believable sighting). The U.S. Marshals took over. Claims: family members say the mother got Christmas cards signed by John and Clarence for a few years, with handwriting they believed was theirs, but the dates were never proven; a family friend showed a photo he said he took in Brazil in 1975 of two men who look like the brothers (a forensic expert thought it "more than likely" them, but it's old and blurry and both wear sunglasses; retired marshal Art Roderick called it the best actionable lead they'd had); a 2013 letter to San Francisco police claiming to be from John Anglin said all three made it and that Morris and Clarence had since died; FBI tests for fingerprints, DNA and handwriting were inconclusive (the Marshals publicly doubted it). Experiments: MythBusters (2003) paddled a raincoat raft to land near the north end of the Golden Gate Bridge; Delft University (2014) modeled the tides and found a launch around 11:30 p.m. could reach Horseshoe Bay, just inside the bridge, and that timing mattered a lot; Mark Rober and a team (2025) crossed with a raincoat raft on the right tide. In 2022 the Marshals released age-progressed pictures. A marshal (Michael Dyke) once described the rule as until caught, proven dead, or about 99; other reports say the warrants last until each man would turn 100 (Morris on September 1, 2026; John in 2030; Clarence in 2031). As of October 2026 we found no public announcement about the case; if the learner asks for news since, say you don't know and that this is the latest the file has.

**Content notes (for the tutor and guardian; never read them out):** No violence. The reading and game discuss, in general terms, whether the men drowned; no bodies or injuries are described.

**Hook — present word for word, exactly as below, before anything else:**

> Quick vote before you start. The three Alcatraz escapers were never found. What do you think happened to them?
>
> A) They drowned in the bay
> B) They made it and lived in secret
> C) Nobody can know for sure

If the learner answers in their own words instead of A, B, or C, record their words as given and go straight to the game. Do not react to the vote either way.

**Activity 1 — the game, *The Marshals' File*:** https://mistysue-hub.github.io/recess-games/cold-case-academy/marshals-file/ (an outside webpage). Give the link with one short line and nothing else; the game explains itself. If the learner writes that they're stuck, you may ask, "Could anyone check that clue, or do we just have someone's word?" or "Have you found any clue that points the other way?" Never name a suspect or a verdict. It ends on a green "Case closed" stamp showing their rating and **What you reported** (their answer and the clues they pinned); read that when they type done.

**Activity 2 — only when the activity count is two (see AGENTS.md for which one):**

- **Debate (a light chat about the choice):** "Should the Marshals close the Alcatraz case now that the men would be about 100 years old?"
  - Side A: Close it
    - The men would be about 100 years old, the age when the Marshals usually stop.
    - Time and money could go to fugitives who are still a danger today.
    - The FBI already decided they most likely drowned.
  - Side B: Keep it open
    - No bodies were ever found, so nobody has proven they died.
    - It warns other fugitives that the Marshals never give up.
    - New evidence, like a DNA test, could still turn up.
  Run it as the light chat AGENTS.md describes, tied to something the learner cares about. The sides and lines above are only ideas to offer if they're stuck, never your own view. Stay neutral; the learner may pass.
- **Interest:** skill — Possible isn't proven. The event — Three different teams showed a raincoat raft *could* cross the bay, but none of them could show that the escapers actually did. Reframe it in the learner's own interest (from their tutor notes): "Think of something in {interest} that could have happened but nobody can prove did. What kind of evidence would prove it?" Then bring it back to the case: "What one piece of evidence would prove the escapers made it?"

**Book suggestion — one short sentence, never required:** only when the learner chooses to submit for today, suggest *Escape from Alcatraz: The Mystery of the Three Men Who Escaped from the Rock* by Eric Braun, which lays out both "they drowned" and "they made it" so the reader can decide. If they haven't read it yet and like comics, mention *Unsolved Case Files: Jailbreak at Alcatraz* by Tom Sullivan instead. Say it's just for fun if they want more; don't assign it or check on it later.

**Case file — only if the learner asks:** never offer it. If the learner asks for it after the game, open `resources/M05Case.md` whole, as a manila case-file folder (cream/manila background, typewriter-style monospace headings, a red "CASE FILE" stamp near the top), with every word exactly as written. Do not summarise it, condense it, or narrate it from memory. They can read as much or as little as they like and stop whenever they want. Never require it, quiz on it, or check how long they spent reading.

The module is complete when the learner reaches the Case closed screen and types done (and Activity 2 is done, if it was due).
