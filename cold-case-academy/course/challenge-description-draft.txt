Become a trainee detective and work real cases from history: heists, escapes and disappearances, some of which nobody has ever solved. Each case starts with a quick vote, then jumps straight into a short illustrated detective game: tap places on a drawn case map, interview witnesses, connect clues on a deduction board, and name a verdict backed by evidence. The game's last screen shows what you reported; type done to Rocky, then choose to submit for the day or start the next case. The real case file is there as an optional extra for kids who ask. Case 1 is a training case with a known answer: the 1911 theft of the Mona Lisa. Then come real unsolved cases. The 1971 D.B. Cooper hijacking: rebuild the night from witness statements, then follow the money a kid found on a riverbank and test five real suspects against the evidence. The 1962 Alcatraz escape: rebuild the escape route from the clues they left, then sort tested facts from untested claims. The 1990 Gardner Museum heist: use a motion-detector record to find the fact that doesn't fit, then separate "who did it" from "where is the art now." An optional case on the Princes in the Tower (1483) weighs 500 years of sources; guardians should know most historians think the two boys were probably killed, though no details are given. The 2025 Louvre crown jewels theft: suspects have been charged but the jewels are still missing, so kids decide what "solved" really means. To graduate, kids write a final case report on the case of their choice: facts, a theory, evidence for and against, and an honest verdict. "We can't tell yet" is an honest answer. No gore or graphic violence. Suggested for ages 10-13. Complete this goal by finishing every module: play each case game through to the Case closed screen and type done to Rocky, then hand in your final report.
