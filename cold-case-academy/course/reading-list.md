# Cold Case Academy — Further Reading (DRAFT)

For ages 10–13. "Before" books build background or excitement and are best read before the module. "After" books go deeper once the learner knows the case. **Grown-up books** are listed separately for parents, guides, or strong older readers. Preview them first, since they're written for adults.

Most titles are in public libraries, and several are on Libby/Sora and Epic.

---

## M01 — Training Case: The Vanishing Mona Lisa

| When | Book | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| After | **The Mona Lisa Vanishes** by Nicholas Day, illus. Brett Helquist (Random House Studio, 2023) | Ages 10–14, grades 5–9. Narrative nonfiction mixing the 1911 theft with Leonardo's life, plus why people fell for wild theories. The best match for this module. |
| Before (younger readers) | **Who Stole Mona Lisa?** by Ruthie Knapp, illus. Jill McElmurry | Picture book told by the painting herself. Too young for most 10–13s, but good for a younger sibling or a quick warm-up. |
| Before | **The Louvre** by Nicole K. Orr | Short kids' nonfiction about the museum itself (on Epic). Useful background for M01 and M09. |

**Grown-up book:** *The Thefts of the Mona Lisa* by Noah Charney.

## M02–M03 — Case 001: D.B. Cooper

| When | Book | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Before or after | **Unsolved Case Files: Escape at 10,000 Feet — D.B. Cooper and the Missing Money** by Tom Sullivan (Balzer + Bray, 2021) | Graphic nonfiction, grades 3–7, about 98 pages. Minute-by-minute flight plus real FBI documents and photos. ALA Top Ten Graphic Novel for Children. Works well **before M02** as a hook. |
| Before (easier) | **The D.B. Cooper Hijacking** by Tom Streissguth | Short and simple (Lexile about 490L, on Epic). Good for a reluctant reader. |
| After | **FBI history page: "D.B. Cooper Hijacking"** (fbi.gov) | Not a book, but kids love seeing the real FBI write-up, the plane ticket, and the unused parachute. |

**Grown-up book:** *Skyjack: The Hunt for D.B. Cooper* by Geoffrey Gray.

## M04–M05 — Case 002: The Alcatraz Escape

| When | Book | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Before or after | **Unsolved Case Files: Jailbreak at Alcatraz — Frank Morris & the Anglin Brothers' Great Escape** by Tom Sullivan (Balzer + Bray, 2021) | Graphic nonfiction, ages 8–12, about 112 pages. ALA Top Ten Graphic Novel for Children. No gore. |
| After | **Escape from Alcatraz: The Mystery of the Three Men Who Escaped from the Rock** by Eric Braun (Capstone, 2017) | Narrative nonfiction, ages 9–12, 113 pages. Lays out both "they drowned" and "they made it." A strong match for **M05**. |
| After | **Escape from Alcatraz** (Unsolved series) by Dinah Williams | Kids' nonfiction on the same case. *I couldn't confirm its age range; check before assigning.* |
| Before (place) | **Where Is Alcatraz?** by Nico Medina (Who HQ) | The island's whole history, not just the escape. Easy, popular series. |
| Just for fun (fiction) | **The Alcatraz Escape** (Book Scavenger #3) by Jennifer Chambliss Bertman | A puzzle-filled mystery novel set on Alcatraz. Not about the 1962 case, but great for kids who caught the "codes and clues" bug. |

**Grown-up book:** *Escape from Alcatraz* by J. Campbell Bruce (1963; the book the Clint Eastwood film was based on).

## M06–M07 — Case 003: The Gardner Museum Heist

| When | Book | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Before or after | **Unsolved Case Files: The 500 Million Dollar Heist — Isabella Stewart Gardner and Thirteen Missing Masterpieces** by Tom Sullivan (Balzer + Bray, 2022) | Graphic nonfiction, ages 8–12. Third in the same series, so kids who liked M02–M05 will already know the style. |
| After (fiction) | **Chasing Vermeer** by Blue Balliett (Scholastic) | A middle-grade mystery about a stolen Vermeer painting, with puzzles and codes woven in. The Gardner heist also took a Vermeer, so this pairs perfectly with M07. |
| After | **Gardner Museum's online "Stolen Art" pages and virtual tour** (gardnermuseum.org) | Shows the empty frames still hanging in the Dutch Room. |

**Grown-up book:** *The Gardner Heist* by Ulrich Boser.

## M08 — Case 004: The Princes in the Tower (optional)

| When | Book | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| After | **The Little Princes in the Tower** by William W. Lace (Mysterious Deaths series, 1996) | Ages 8–12, 112 pages, with family trees and a timeline. Older and may need a library request. **Guardian note:** the series title and topic deal with the likely deaths of two boys. |
| Before | **Historic Royal Palaces schools video: "The Princes in the Tower"** (hrp.org.uk) | Short video with built-in questions (KS2–KS3). The gentlest way in. |

**Grown-up book:** *The Princes in the Tower* by Alison Weir.

## M09 — Case 005: Seven Minutes at the Louvre (2025)

This case is too recent for kids' books. Use these instead:

| When | Resource | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Before | **Britannica: "What happened during the 2025 Louvre heist?"** | A short, factual summary at a good reading level. |
| After | **The Mona Lisa Vanishes** (see M01) | Re-reading the 1911 chapters makes a great compare-and-contrast. Two Louvre thefts, 114 years apart. |
| After | **The Louvre** by Nicole K. Orr (see M01) | Background on the museum and its treasures. |

*Check for new kids' coverage closer to launch, since books on this case may appear in 2026–27.*

## M10 — Final Report: Your Verdict

Books about how real investigators think, for kids who want to keep detecting:

| When | Book | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Before | **Scene of the Crime: Tracking Down Criminals with Forensic Science** by HP Newquist (Viking/Penguin) | Middle-grade history of fingerprints, DNA and crime labs. Connects to the M01 fingerprint mix-up. *Preview first: it covers real crime-solving cases, so check chapters for your learner.* |
| Before | **CSI Expert! Forensic Science for Kids** by Karen K. Schulz (Prufrock, grades 5–8) | Hands-on activities: fingerprints, handwriting, evidence. Good to pair with writing the final report. |

## Podcasts (any module)
- **The Mystery Kids Podcast** (Tess O'Driscoll): kid-friendly "Unsolved Mysteries," including a Princes in the Tower episode.
- **Mysteries in History – For Kids** (Noah Gorton): short episodes, also covers the Princes in the Tower.
- **History's Little Mysteries:** 5–7 minute unsolved history stories.

### Where these go in the course
Each module file's **Resources and surfaces** table gets a row: *"Further reading (guardian, optional)"* pointing to that module's section above. Rocky can suggest one "after" book when a learner finishes a module and says they want more. He never assigns it as required.
