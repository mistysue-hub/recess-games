# M04 notes (Rocky only — never open on screen or read out)

## Order for this module
Play the game first, right after the hook vote, before the reading. It works best unspoiled. Do not explain trick cards before they play. Then the reading, look back, talk-back and the rest as usual.

## Hook answer
False. In the 1949 experiment, trick cards took about four times longer to recognize, and almost everyone first saw them as normal cards. They were also asked how sure they were (0 to 100). If they were very sure and wrong, say so kindly: that feeling of being sure is what the experiment found.

## Talk-back (one at a time, after the look-back)
1. What did most students say when they first saw a red six of spades?
   Answer: they called it a normal card, either the six of hearts or the six of spades.
2. Why did trick cards get easier after a student caught the first one?
   Answer: once they knew trick cards existed, they expected them, so they could see them.

## Side View question
Core: Hanson said what you see depends on what you already know. Think of an X-ray: a doctor and someone who has never studied medicine look at the same picture. Do they see the same thing? Can you think of another example?
Extended: A doctor and a first-year student look at the same X-ray. Whose observation should count as evidence, and could the student ever see something the doctor's training hides?
Opinion question: no right answer. Stay neutral.

## Game: Odd Card Out
Cards flash for a short moment, then get covered. The learner taps what card they saw from four choices (the four suits of the card's rank; no colors are named). Two of the ten cards are trick cards with the wrong color, and the game doesn't say so. After a correct pick the game asks "Was anything odd about the card?" (a trick card counts as noticed only if they say something looked off). Each card flashes again, a bit longer each time, until they get it right (up to two flashes). Core flashes are longer than Extended. The finish screen shows how many normal and trick cards they named on the first flash, and how many they never named, alongside the 1949 results. The last screen is "Notebook closed."
Stuck nudge: "Look at the color as well as the shape." Never name the card.

## Activity 2 (Core: only if the activity count is two; Extended: runs by default)
Core: Ask the learner about a time they misread or misheard something because they expected something else (a word, a sign, a song lyric's meaning, a face in a crowd). What made them finally notice?
Extended: Say: "In 2015, a team of researchers tried to repeat 100 published psychology experiments. Nearly all the originals had reported a clear result; only about a third of the repeats found it again." Ask: "Does that sound like a field in crisis, or a field catching its own mistakes? What would Kuhn look for to tell?" If they want more: many researchers responded by changing habits, like announcing their plans before running a study and using bigger groups. The card experiment from this module was not part of that project, and you don't know whether it has been repeated; don't say. Stay neutral.

## Anomaly Log

Extended: "If this module had to lose one sentence or example, which would you cut, and why? Add it to your log, then tell me one thing you wrote."
