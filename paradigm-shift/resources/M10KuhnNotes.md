## Hook answer
No single right answer (note how sure they were). The look-back: the reading says Popper and Kuhn agreed on a lot (science goes through big changes and isn't just piling up facts) but disagreed sharply on how scientists should act: Popper said always try to break your theory, so a failure is a reason to ditch it; Kuhn said commit long enough to find out what it can do, so a failure is a puzzle to fix first.

## Talk-back (one at a time, after the look-back)
1. What's one strength of Kuhn's view and one strength of Popper's?
   Any fair answer. Examples: Kuhn: sticking with Newton found Neptune. Popper: always testing catches mistakes.
2. Which of Kuhn's five values matters most to you, and why?
   Opinion. The five: accuracy, consistency, scope, simplicity, fruitfulness.

## Side View question
Core: Picture a science fair where every project is checked by kids who disagree with each other. Would that make the winners more trustworthy? Does that sound like Kuhn's side, Popper's side, or something new? (Longino said science is trustworthy when a community welcomes criticism.)
Extended: Longino's four conditions are public places to criticize, a community that responds, shared standards, and equal say. Which of the four does this course, with its Anomaly Log, meet least well?

## Facts to get right
- July 1965, Bedford College, London. Feyerabend couldn't attend. Book: Criticism and the Growth of Knowledge (1970), edited by Lakatos and Musgrave. Popper's paper: "Normal Science and its Dangers."
- Kuhn's five values: lecture 1973, published 1977.
- Kuhn's Copernicus example: not more accurate; clashed with accepted physics (Earth doesn't move); simpler for the backward loops (one circle per planet instead of two).
- Never say Kuhn thought science is irrational or just a crowd.

## Activity 2 (only if the activity count is two; always at Extended): formal debate
Motion: "Scientists should defend their theories until the evidence forces them to change." The learner argues their side (one or two reasons). Then you steel-man the other side: state the strongest case for it in two or three sentences, using only ideas from the reading, and ask them to answer it. Then they argue the other side. You stay neutral and never say who won.
