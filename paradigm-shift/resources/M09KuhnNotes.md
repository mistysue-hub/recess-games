# M09 notes (Rocky only — never open on screen or read out)

## Hook answer
True. Scientists and teachers still use older models, knowing they are wrong in detail, when they're good enough for the job: the Bohr model is still drawn in classrooms, and chemists use Dalton's whole-number ratios every day.

## Talk-back (one at a time, after the look-back)
1. Why do teachers still draw the Bohr model if it isn't the newest?
   Answer: it's simple to picture and good enough for many jobs, like explaining energy levels.
2. Kuhn compared science to evolution. What does "progress away from, not toward" mean?
   Answer: science gets better at solving puzzles than before, but isn't necessarily heading to one final, perfect truth.

## Side View question
Core: Think of a map of your town that gets better every year, with more streets and fewer mistakes. Is that getting closer to the real town, or just a more useful map? (Popper leaned toward closer to the truth; Kuhn toward better at solving puzzles.)
Extended: Bohr's atom is wrong in detail, yet teachers still draw it. Does a model that's wrong but useful count as progress?
Opinion question: no right answer. Stay neutral.

## Facts to get right
- Dalton early 1800s (book 1808); Thomson found the electron 1897, model 1904 (he didn't call it "plum pudding"); gold-foil key result 1909; Rutherford's nucleus 1911; Bohr 1913; Schrödinger 1926.
- Kuhn loss (Extended): Descartes's vortices explained why all planets orbit the same way; Newton's gravity didn't. Call it "an example often given," not Kuhn's own.

## Activity 2 (Core: only if the activity count is two; Extended: runs by default)
Core: Say: "Until about 2012, computer programs for recognizing pictures were built by hand: a programmer wrote rules for what to look for. Then a new kind of program called a **neural network** learned on its own from millions of examples. In just a few years, almost everyone switched to the new kind. The old way isn't totally gone, but it's rare now." Ask: "Does that sound like a tiny tweak, or a bigger change where the whole idea of 'how we do it' flipped? Which word in Kuhn's story fits best: puzzle, anomaly or revolution?" Don't add other facts about AI. Stay neutral.
Extended: Say: "Before 2012, most image-recognition programs relied on features that researchers designed by hand. In 2012 a neural network called AlexNet won a major image-recognition contest by a wide margin, and within a few years most vision researchers had switched to neural networks." Ask: "Using Kuhn's ideas (new standard examples, new vocabulary, new ideas of what counts as a good answer), is that a paradigm shift, or just a better tool?" If time: "What might have been lost, a Kuhn loss?" Don't add other facts about AI. Stay neutral.
