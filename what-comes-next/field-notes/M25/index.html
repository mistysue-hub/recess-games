The best kind of decision

Look at you! You've explored, planned, applied and hunted. Now the offers are coming in (or about to), and you get to make one of the most exciting decisions of your life so far: where you're saying YES.

This module helps you compare your options fairly, ask for a better deal when it makes sense, and say yes (and no) like a pro. (Rocky has prepared a celebratory calendar entry. It just says "🎉 YES DAY 🎉" in every color he owns.)

2

The final Fit check

Pull out your Fit Scorecard from M16, M17 and M24. For each offer, look at all three scores:

  • Academic Fit: will you learn and grow there?
  • Social Fit: will you feel at home?
  • Money Fit: does the real cost work for you and your family?

Then do the gut check from M14: imagine you've said yes and it's your first week. How do you feel? Relieved and excited? Or quietly disappointed? Your gut is data too. If the numbers and your gut disagree, ask why. One of them knows something.

3

Comparing offers in every lane

🎓 College

Line up your aid offers side by side, the way you did in Offer Showdown (M24): free money, earned money and loans, for all four years, not just the first. Visit admitted-student days if you can. Many colleges ask for your decision and deposit around early May (May 1 is common), but dates vary, so check each one. You accept one college. Most colleges expect you to deposit at only one, and some can cancel your admission if you deposit at more. If you applied Early Decision and got in, that agreement is binding (unless the aid offer truly doesn't work for your family, so talk to the college if that happens).

One more thing: most offers depend on finishing senior year well. Colleges ask for your final transcript, and they can take back (rescind) an offer if your grades drop a lot. Coast a little if you must, but don't crash.

🔧 Trade School / Apprenticeship

Compare the total cost (or the wages, for an apprenticeship), the start date, the schedule, the hands-on hours, and the program's placement record. If you got into an apprenticeship and a trade school, remember that one pays you to learn.

💼 Career

A job offer is more than the hourly pay. Compare pay, hours, benefits (health insurance, paid time off, retirement), training, the commute, and room to grow (M10). A job that pays a little less but trains you, pays for classes, or promotes from within might be the better deal.

🚀 Work for yourself

Time for a go/no-go check on your business. Did your tests (M11) find real customers who paid? Do you have enough money to cover a slow start? What's your plan if it takes longer than you hoped? "Not yet" is a perfectly good answer, and many people start on the side while studying or working.

🌍 Crossing borders

Saying yes may start more paperwork. International students usually need a form from their school (an I-20 or DS-2019) and must pay the SEVIS fee before applying for a student visa. There are other visa fees too (a new one started in 2025–26), so check the current list. Studyinthestates.dhs.gov and travel.state.gov explain the steps, and wait times change, so check them. Start right away, because visa steps take time.

4

Asking for a better offer

Here's something many people never learn: you can often ask. Politely asking for a better offer is called negotiating, and employers, programs and colleges expect some people to do it. You might ask about:

  • Pay (for a job)
  • Start date or schedule
  • Training or certifications
  • Aid (that's the appeal from M24)

How to ask:

  1. Thank them and say you're excited.
  2. Ask for one thing, with a reason ("Could the starting pay be $17? That's what similar jobs in our area are paying.").
  3. Then stop talking and listen. The pause is part of it.
  4. Get the final offer in writing.

They might say yes, no, or "let me check." All three are fine. It's rare for a reasonable employer to take back an offer over one polite question. Some offers can't change (many union apprenticeships have set pay scales, for example), and that's okay too.

Know your walk-away line before you ask: the thing you truly need to say yes. Decide it ahead of time, so you don't have to decide under pressure.

5

Saying yes

  • Accept in writing by the deadline, with any deposit or forms they need.
  • Read what you're agreeing to, with a parent.
  • Celebrate! Tell your Cheer Squad (M22). They've been waiting to hear.
6

Saying no (nicely)

Decline the offers you're not taking promptly, so someone else can get the spot. Be kind and brief: thank them, say you've accepted another offer, and wish them well. The world is small, and the person reading your email might be your boss in five years.

7

Planning your next raise

Start a "win list" on day one: things you learned, problems you solved, compliments you got. When it's time to ask for a raise or a promotion, you'll have proof ready.

10

🍁 In Canada?

There's no binding "Early Decision" in Canada. Many schools make early offers based on your grade 11 and 12 marks. Ontario universities can't make you accept before early June; other schools set their own dates (some ask you to answer early offers by around early May), so check each offer. Everything else here (comparing, asking, saying yes and no nicely) works the same.
