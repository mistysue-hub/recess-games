Beat the summer slide

Here's something that surprises people: every year, some students who've been accepted and are all set to go don't make it to day one. Counselors call it "summer melt." It usually isn't because the student changed their mind. More often, a form, a payment or a deadline slipped through the cracks, or a surprise cost popped up and nobody was around to help figure it out.

The fix is simple: keep your Command Center (M26) running all summer. Check your email and portals every week, even on vacation. Watch for:

  • Orientation and registration sign-ups
  • Housing forms and deposits
  • Financial aid follow-ups (sometimes a school asks you to prove the information on your FAFSA, a step called verification)
  • Health forms, like immunization records
  • Placement tests or class sign-ups
  • Your final transcript and proof of graduation (for homeschoolers, your parents send these)

If something confuses you, call or email the office right away. Offices are there to help, and asking early is how you stay on track.

Your launch checklist

Work backward from your first day (M19 style):

  • 3 months out: confirm your start date, finish any forms, and make a money plan for the first semester or first month (M28).
  • 1 month out: sort out housing and transportation, and make a packing or gear list.
  • 1 week out: do a practice run of your commute or route, set up your Command Center for the new schedule, and get some good sleep.
  • Day one: arrive early, bring what they asked for, say hi to at least three people, and ask questions.
4

The first few weeks

Big changes take getting used to, especially after homeschooling. Expect a few surprises:

  • Fixed schedules: classes, shifts and meetings start on time, whether you're ready or not. Your calendar is your best friend.
  • Big rooms and big crews: you might be one of 200 in a lecture or one of 30 on a job site. Sit near the front, learn a few names, and introduce yourself to the instructor or supervisor.
  • Finding your people: say yes to things (M17, M28). The first few weeks are a great time to meet people, since everyone's new and looking for friends too.
  • It's okay to feel off at first. Homesickness and nerves are normal and usually fade. If they don't, reach out (M27).

Cars and the road

  • Car insurance is required in nearly every state. Rates are often high for new drivers, so compare quotes and ask about good-student discounts.
  • After an accident: get safe first (and call 911 if anyone's hurt), swap names, insurance and plate numbers, take photos, and report it to the police if needed and to your insurance company.
  • If you're pulled over: pull over safely, turn on the inside light at night, keep your hands on the wheel, and stay calm and polite. Tell the officer before you reach for your license or registration.

Everyday living

  • Food: make a grocery list and learn five easy meals. Cooking at home saves a lot.
  • Laundry: read the labels, wash darks and lights apart, and don't overstuff the machine.
  • Phone plans and subscriptions: compare plans, and watch for free trials that auto-renew. Cancel what you don't use.

🎓 College survival kit

  • Placement tests: some colleges have you take a math or language test before you pick classes. Ask if yours does.
  • Add/drop and withdraw deadlines: early in the term, you can usually switch classes with no mark on your record. Later, withdrawing usually leaves a W on your transcript. Find both dates in week one.
  • Keep your aid: to keep federal aid, you need passing grades and enough finished credits, called Satisfactory Academic Progress. Low grades can also land you on academic probation, a warning period to bring them up.
  • Campus safety: save the campus police number, learn the safety escort or alert app, and know where the Title IX office is (it handles sex discrimination, including sexual harassment and assault).

Tell your people

Your network helped you get here, so close the loop:

  • Thank your Cheer Squad (M22). Tell them where you're going and thank them for helping.
  • Update your people list (M26) and share your news with mentors, the people you interviewed (M08), and your Recess crew.
  • Update your résumé or portfolio (M21) with what you've accomplished.

6

🎓 Celebrate your graduation!

You did it. Homeschool graduation is a real graduation, and it deserves a real celebration. Some ideas:

  • A ceremony at home, with your co-op, or through a homeschool group (many states have homeschool graduation events).
  • Your parents presenting your diploma (M20) in front of family and friends.
  • A party with your Cheer Squad, mentors and the people who helped along the way.
  • A "look back" display: projects, photos and your Experience Log highlights.

However you celebrate, take a moment to be proud. You planned your own path. That's a big deal.

7

Launch in every lane

🎓 College

Register for orientation early (sessions can fill up), check your student portal and email every week, and finish financial aid, housing and health forms. If your aid package changes, contact the financial aid office right away.

🔧 Trade School / Apprenticeship

Confirm your start date, schedule and any required tools, boots or safety gear. Ask what to bring on day one. If you're starting an apprenticeship, ask how your hours will be tracked.

💼 Career

Confirm your start date, time, where to go and who to ask for. Ask about first-day paperwork (M23). If you're heading into the military, stay in touch with your recruiter, keep up with any fitness or paperwork requirements, and bring a parent to any meeting.

🚀 Work for yourself

Pick a launch day, tell your network, and set up your work schedule and money tracking (M28). Plan your first month's goals, and celebrate your first sale.

🌍 Crossing borders

Book your visa appointment early (travel.state.gov lists US embassy wait times), keep your travel documents together, confirm your arrival dates with your school or program, and check in with the international office when you arrive.
