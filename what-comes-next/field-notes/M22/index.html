You don't have to say it all yourself

Telling your own story is great (that was M21). But some things sound better coming from someone else. "I'm reliable" is nice. A shop manager saying "In a year, she was never late once, and she trained our new hires" is way nicer.

That's your Cheer Squad: the people who write recommendation letters, act as references, and vouch for you. Today you'll figure out who belongs on it, how to ask them the right way, and how to make their job easy. (Rocky volunteered to be on your squad. Sadly, colleges want letters from humans who have met you in person. He's taking it well.)

2

Who can be on your squad

For homeschoolers, the best recommenders are usually adults outside your family who've seen you learn or work. Look at your People Map from M08 and your Experience Log:

  • Co-op, tutor or online-class teachers
  • Dual-enrollment instructors
  • Your Recess guides and mentors who know your work
  • Coaches, music teachers, scout or club leaders
  • Employers, internship supervisors and volunteer coordinators
  • Clients you've done work for (🚀)
  • Faith or community leaders who know you well

Your parent usually writes the counselor letter (M20), but for other recommendations, programs want someone outside the family.

3

What makes a great recommender

Fame doesn't matter. A letter from a senator who met you once is weaker than one from a co-op teacher who saw you work all year. Pick people who:

  1. Know you well, not just your name.
  2. Know you recently, ideally in the last year or two.
  3. Saw you in a setting that fits what you're applying for (a chemistry teacher for an engineering program, a manager for a job).
  4. Can give examples. Specific stories make letters strong, just like in M21.
  5. Will actually do it on time. Reliable beats impressive.
4

How to ask (the right way)

  1. Ask early. Give them at least a month before the deadline, more during busy seasons. (This is a "waiting on someone" task from M19. The Form Monster loves late asks.)
  2. Ask personally, in person, by video or by phone if you can, with a parent's okay. An email is fine as a follow-up or if that's how you usually talk.
  3. Make it easy to say no. Try: "Do you feel you know my work well enough to write a strong letter for me?" If they hesitate, thank them and ask someone else. A lukewarm letter doesn't help you.
  4. Send a "brag sheet" once they say yes: what you're applying for, the deadline, how to submit, your résumé, and two or three things you'd love them to mention (with examples to jog their memory).
5

After they say yes

  • Send a friendly reminder about a week before the deadline.
  • Say thank you with a real note once it's submitted. Handwritten is a lovely touch.
  • Tell them what happened. People who helped you really want to know how it turned out, and it keeps the relationship strong for next time.
6

How each lane uses your squad

🎓 College

Many colleges ask for one or two teacher recommendations plus the counselor letter. For homeschoolers, "teachers" can be co-op, dual-enrollment or online-class instructors. Some colleges also accept an other recommender (a coach, employer or mentor), and some have extra rules for homeschoolers, so check each college. On the Common App you'll be asked whether you waive your right to see your letters later. Many counselors suggest waiving it, because it shows colleges the letters are honest. You finish that FERPA section first, then invite each recommender by email from your Common App account, so tell them to watch for the email.

🔧 Trade School / Apprenticeship

Programs often ask for references: names and contact information of people who can talk about your work ethic and skills. Ask each person first, and tell them when someone might call.

💼 Career

Employers usually want two or three references, often later in the hiring process. Pick people who've seen you work (a boss, a supervisor, a volunteer coordinator). Always ask permission first, and give them a heads-up when you've listed them.

🚀 Work for yourself

Your squad is your happy clients. Ask them for a short testimonial you can share on your portfolio, and ask permission to use their name. Only real words from real people. Never write fake reviews.

🌍 Crossing borders

Schools in other countries may want references in a specific format, on letterhead, or translated. Check early, because your recommenders will need extra time.

7

🏡 Homeschool Edge

You probably know your teachers, coaches and mentors better than many students know theirs. Smaller groups mean the adults in your life have really seen you work. That makes for detailed, powerful letters.

8

🔭 Lane Peek

Read a lane section you skipped. If a friend on that path asked who should vouch for them, what would you tell them?

9

🍁 In Canada?

Canadian universities ask for fewer recommendation letters, but homeschoolers are often the exception: McGill, for example, asks homeschoolers for a confidential letter from an impartial adult. Big scholarships, some programs, and most jobs and apprenticeships still want references, so build your Cheer Squad just the same.

10

👪 Ask Your Parents

  1. "Will you write my counselor letter, and when should I ask you for it?"
  2. "Is it okay if I contact [name] to ask for a recommendation?"
  3. "Who do you think knows my work best outside our family?"
