Welcome to the fun part

At some point soon, it will happen. You'll be minding your own business, maybe eating a sandwich, and an adult will lean in and ask The Question:

"So! What are you doing after graduation?"

Aunts ask it. Neighbors ask it. Your dentist asks it while both his hands are in your mouth, which feels a little unfair.

If your honest answer right now is "I have no idea," congratulations: you are completely normal. Most adults had no idea at your age either, and plenty of them changed their minds two or three times before landing somewhere they love. (Ask around. You'll hear some great stories.)

Here's the good news nobody says loudly enough: this is one of the most exciting moments of your whole life. The whole world is about to open up, and you get to choose where to go. What do you want to learn? Where do you want to live? What do you want to build, fix, create, or change? You get to decide.

And as a homeschooler, you're walking in with a head start. You already know how to learn on your own, chase an interest way down the rabbit hole, manage your time, and talk with adults without hiding behind a potted plant. Colleges, training programs and employers love all of that.

Best of all, you're not doing this alone. You've got your family, and you've got your Recess crew: a growing community of friends, guides and mentors who are genuinely rooting for you. Think of them as your pit crew for the big race. (Rocky is the one waving the calendar.)

This course will help you:

  • discover what you love and what you're great at,
  • explore every path out there (there are way more than you think!),
  • choose a direction that fits the life you want,
  • and make it happen, with every deadline, form, and step under control.

About those forms. Somewhere out there lives the Form Monster, a creature made entirely of paperwork and due dates. It has tripped up a lot of people with big dreams. Homeschoolers face a few extra paper pieces, like a transcript your family creates and recommendations from people outside your family. But don't worry. We have a plan, a calendar, and Rocky, who gets way too excited about calendars. (He knows. He's working on it. He is not working on it very hard.)

Where are you on the clock?

In homeschool, your family decides when you graduate, so Rocky will ask for your planned graduation date. Early, on time, or flexible: all welcome! Your timeline changes how fast we move, not how far you can go.

🌲 18 or more months out: Long Runway. You have time to explore, try things, and change your mind as often as you like. Take the scenic route!

⛰️ 12–17 months out: Steady Climb. Still lots of time, and this is when the fun stuff starts: visits, test dates, summer programs.

🍂 9–11 months out: Senior Fall. Applications are happening now, which means your future is getting real. Let's go!

⏰ Under 9 months: Crunch Time. Deep breath. Tons of doors are still wide open: community colleges, apprenticeships, rolling-admission schools, jobs that train you, a planned gap year. We'll move fast and grab a great one. Think of it less as "panic" and more as "action movie montage."

Once you know your date, pick your track below. (Not sure? Ask your parents, or ask Rocky to help you count the months.)

Three lanes, endless destinations

Every path after high school fits into one of three lanes. You can explore one, two, or all three at once, and switch anytime without losing any progress. Think of it like a GPS: take a different turn and it just says "recalculating," not "WRONG."

🎓 College / University

Four-year colleges and universities, community colleges (a smart, affordable start and a great springboard to a four-year school), even universities overseas. Great if you love digging deep into a subject, or want a career that needs a degree, like teaching, engineering, medicine, or research.

🔧 Trade School / Apprenticeship

Trade and technical schools, certificate programs, and apprenticeships where you get paid while you learn. (Yes, really. Someone pays you to learn. Wild.) Electricians, welders, HVAC techs, medical assistants, chefs, aircraft mechanics, and many more. Great if you love working with your hands, solving real-world problems, and seeing results fast.

💼 Career

Jump straight into work, join a company that trains you, serve in the military, take a planned gap year, or 🚀 work for yourself (start a business, freelance, build games or websites). You can turn on 🚀 alongside any lane. Great if you want experience, income, and independence right away, and like learning by doing.

Notice what's not here: a ranking. No lane is "better" than another. The best path is the one that lights you up and fits the life you want.

🔭 Pro tip: peek at the other lanes too. Even after you pick a lane, Rocky will offer quick 2-minute peeks at the others. Two reasons: lots of people change course at some point (totally normal), and one day a friend, sibling or cousin might need exactly what you know. Be the person who can say, "Oh, I know how that works!"

5

🌟 Your trail, your pace

Before we go anywhere, take a second to notice how far you've already come. Every course you finished, every project, job, hobby and hard thing you stuck with got you here. Be proud of that.

And one rule for the whole trail: don't compare your trail to anyone else's. A friend might seem sure of their plan, or someone online might look way ahead. They're on a different trail with a different map. Your future is exciting on every path, including the one you're about to discover.

6

Rocky keeps it real (in a good way)

Rocky is your biggest fan, and he's honest. If a program costs more than the budget, needs grades you don't have yet, or has a deadline that's already passed, he'll tell you, kindly, and then help you find another exciting route to the same goal. There almost always is one. Detours are just bonus scenery.

🍁 In Canada?

Welcome aboard! Everything here works for you too. Wherever a step works differently in Canada (like student aid, apprenticeships or application dates), Rocky will show you a 🍁 note or open a Canadian version of the page. Your graduation timing follows your province's homeschool rules, so ask your parents what applies where you live.

8

👪 Ask Your Parents

In homeschool, your parents are also your school counselor (and principal, and lunch lady, and probably IT department). You'll need their help at a few key moments: building your transcript, writing a counselor letter, sharing info for financial-aid forms, and talking through big decisions. Whenever you need something from them, you'll see an Ask Your Parents box like this one, and Rocky can help you write a quick note to share. For everything else (ideas, encouragement, "wait, how did you figure out what to do?"), your Recess guides, mentors and friends are great people to ask too.

For now, just two things:

  1. Tell them you've started planning what comes next. (Prepare for possible happy dancing.)
  2. Ask them your planned graduation date, if you're not sure, so Rocky can put you on the right track.
