Meet the Form Monster

Every path after graduation comes with paperwork: applications, forms, registrations, deadlines. Rocky calls it the Form Monster. It isn't evil. It's just hungry, it never sleeps, and it loves eating students who say "I'll do it later."

The good news: the Form Monster has one weakness. A calendar. Today you'll build one calendar that holds every deadline in every lane you're following, and you'll learn the trick that beats the monster every time: plan backward.

(Yes, this is Rocky's favourite module. He's been colour-coding his excitement for weeks. In both official languages.)

2

Three kinds of dates

Not all dates are the same. Your calendar needs all three:

  1. Deadlines: the last possible day. Applications, registrations, scholarship and student-aid dates.
  2. Do dates: the day you actually start. Work backward from the deadline: how long will it take, plus a cushion for surprises?
  3. Waiting-on-someone dates: anything that depends on another person. A reference letter, a parent's income details for student aid, a transcript, a school's reply.

That third kind is where most people get eaten. You can't control how fast other people move, so start the "waiting on someone" tasks first. Ask referees at least a month before the deadline. Ask your parents for paperwork early. Then do your own parts while you wait.

Homeschoolers get extra ones. McGill, for example, asks for a home educator's statement and a confidential letter from an impartial referee, and other schools want course descriptions or a portfolio. Ask early.

3

⚠️ Early-deadline paths

Some paths start long before grade 12 ends, and they surprise people every year:

  • ROTP (the Canadian Armed Forces' paid-degree plan): you study at RMC or a civilian university, get paid, and then serve. Forces.ca says to start in September of your final year. Pilot, Air Combat Systems Officer and Aerospace Control Officer applicants face an earlier deadline, around mid-December; most others around late January.
  • Loran Award: a big national scholarship for students graduating high school. It closes around mid-October of grade 12, so you need to start the summer before. Homeschool eligibility isn't spelled out, so a parent should contact Loran to ask.
  • Schulich Leader Scholarships (STEM): high schools (and CEGEPs) nominate students, around late January. Homeschoolers should ask the program early how they can be nominated.
  • Sports: for Canadian universities (U SPORTS) or colleges (CCAA), you usually need to get admitted and keep your marks up, and many athletes start talking with coaches in grades 11 and 12. If you're aiming at an NCAA school in the US, courses can count from grade 9, so start by grade 9 or 10.

If any of these interest you even a little, note it on the page today. Finding out late is the one plot twist we really want to avoid.

4

Deadlines in every lane

🎓 College and university

  • There's no binding Early Decision in Canada. Many universities make early offers based on your grade 11 and 12 marks, so applying earlier can mean hearing earlier. Homeschoolers' files often take longer to review, which is one more reason to apply early.
  • One application system per province, usually: OUAC (Ontario universities), ontariocolleges.ca (Ontario colleges), EducationPlannerBC, ApplyAlberta, and SRAM or other regional services for Quebec CEGEPs. Some schools use their own portal. Read each school's homeschool page early, because extra documents may have their own dates.
  • Big dates run from around mid-January (Ontario universities, UBC) to around early March (Quebec CEGEPs, University of Alberta). The heads-up calendar below has the list.
  • Application fees vary by system and school. Check the current fee, and ask whether any waiver exists.
  • Student aid goes through your province (OSAP in Ontario, StudentAid BC, Alberta Student Aid, and Quebec's own Aide financière aux études). You apply once a year, it's free, and you'll need a SIN. Many families file once you've accepted an offer, but check when your province opens applications.
  • Responding to offers: Ontario universities can't require you to accept or pay a deposit before about June 1. Other schools set their own dates (the University of Alberta, for example, asks for answers to early offers around May 1).

🔧 Trade School / Apprenticeship

Apprenticeships are run by your province or territory. In many provinces you can register any time once you find an employer sponsor, so finding a sponsor is the slow part. Start a year ahead. College trade and pre-apprenticeship programs have set intakes (fall, winter or several times a year), and popular ones fill up. In Quebec, construction trades usually start with a DEP (vocational diploma), which has its own application dates.

💼 Career

Lots of summer jobs hire in winter and early spring: Canada Summer Jobs postings on Job Bank, Parks Canada, provincial wildfire crews, camps. For the Canadian Armed Forces, applying involves several steps (the CFAT aptitude test, a medical, an interview), so give yourself months, not weeks.

🚀 Work for yourself

Set launch milestones: test your idea, register your business name if your province requires it (with a parent), set up a way to get paid, and plan a launch date. Markets and game jams have their own sign-up dates too.

🌍 Crossing borders

Heading to the US? You'll follow the US calendar (early applications around November, regular ones around January, decisions around May 1). Canadian citizens don't need a visa stamp to study there, but still need the school's I-20 form and must pay the SEVIS fee before going. The US version of this module has the full US deadline list.

Build your calendar

Here's how to set it up:

  1. Pick one place for everything: a paper planner, a calendar app, or both. One. Not five.
  2. Add every deadline from your lanes, your testing plan (M18) and your goals (M15).
  3. Work backward from each deadline to set a do date, plus a cushion of a week or two.
  4. Mark the "waiting on someone" tasks and schedule them first.
  5. Set reminders a week before and a day before each big date.
  6. Check it every week. Sunday evening works great for a lot of people.

Two application habits: make a professional email address (something like firstname.lastname) and use it only for applications, so nothing important gets buried under game updates. After you apply, check each school's applicant portal weekly for "missing documents" or "outstanding items," because one missing form can quietly hold up your whole file.

You'll build a full system to keep it all running in M26, the Command Center. For now, get every date in one place.

🏡 Homeschool Edge

Your flexible schedule is a superpower here. You can work on applications on a Tuesday morning, call admissions offices while they're open, and visit campuses when other students are in class. Just make sure flexible doesn't turn into "later." The monster loves "later."
