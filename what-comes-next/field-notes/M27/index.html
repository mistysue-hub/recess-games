Every good story has one

Here's something true about every person you admire: their path didn't go exactly as planned. They got a "no," changed their mind, lost a job, or took a detour, and the detour turned out to matter.

Plans change. That's not failure. That's life doing its thing. Today is about what to do when it happens: how to bounce back, find a new route, and take care of yourself along the way. (Rocky has rerouted more trips than he can count. He says "recalculating..." is one of the most hopeful words he knows.)

2

When you hear "no"

Rejection stings. A college says no, an apprenticeship picks someone else, a job never calls back. It's okay to feel disappointed, and it's okay to take a day.

Then remember:

  • A "no" is about one decision, not about you. Programs turn down great people every year for reasons that have nothing to do with them, like limited spots.
  • Ask for feedback when you can, especially for apprenticeships and jobs. "Is there anything I could do to be a stronger applicant next time?"
  • Many doors open more than once. Apprenticeships reopen, jobs repost, and students transfer to colleges that turned them down the first time.
3

Waitlists and "maybe"

A waitlist means "not yet, but maybe." If you're waitlisted somewhere you'd love to go:

  1. Say yes to the waitlist (it's usually a form).
  2. Accept another offer anyway, by its deadline, so you have a sure place.
  3. Send a short update if the school allows it: new grades, awards, and why it's still your top choice.

A deferral (common with early applications) means they'll look again later. Keep your grades up and send an update if they invite one.

4

Other plot twists

  • A job ends or a layoff happens. It happens to good workers. File for unemployment help if you qualify, tell your network (M08), and update your win list (M25).
  • Your business doesn't take off. You learned more in a few months than many people learn in years. That experience counts on a résumé (M21). Many successful owners started over more than once.
  • You change your mind. You might switch majors, programs or lanes, transfer, or take a gap year. That's allowed. Switching lanes never erases what you've learned.
5

Plan B and Plan C

A Plan B isn't a consolation prize. It's another good route to a place you want to be. Here's how to build one:

  1. Sort it out: what can you control, and what can't you? Put your energy into the first list.
  2. Map two routes: a Plan B and a Plan C. They might reach the same goal another way (community college, then transfer) or lead somewhere new.
  3. Name one person from your People Map who could help.
  4. Pick one small next step for this week.
6

Leaning on your people

You don't have to figure out plot twists alone. Your family, your friends, your mentors and your Recess community want to help. Telling someone "this didn't go how I hoped" is brave, and it usually makes things feel lighter.

7

Taking care of you

Big changes are exciting and stressful at the same time. Here's how to look after yourself:

  • Homesickness is normal, even if you're excited. Call home, keep a few routines from home, and give it time. It usually gets easier after the first weeks.
  • Sleep, food and movement are the foundation. When they slip, everything feels harder.
  • Stress basics: break problems into small steps (M26), take a walk, talk to someone, and limit the late-night scrolling.
  • Skip the comparing. Someone else's acceptance, job or timeline isn't a deadline for you. Plenty of people find their best path on the second or third try.
  • Ask for help early. Colleges have counseling centers, many employers have support programs, and a trusted adult is always a good start.

If you're ever feeling hopeless, or thinking about hurting yourself, please reach out right away. You can call or text 988 (the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) any time, day or night, and talk to a trusted adult. You matter, and help is there.

8

Plot twists in every lane

🎓 College

If you don't get in where you hoped, community college plus a transfer agreement (M16) is a great route. If college isn't right after a semester, talk to an advisor and the financial aid office before you leave, because leaving mid-term can mean paying back part of your aid. There may be options like a leave of absence.

Want a gap year before you start? Ask your college about deferring your admission. Many allow it if you share a plan, but your scholarships may not carry over, so ask about those too.

🔧 Trade School / Apprenticeship

If you don't get the apprenticeship, ask how to strengthen your next application. A pre-apprenticeship program, a related job, or a trade school class can make you a stronger candidate.

💼 Career

If a job isn't working out, try to fix it first (talk to your manager, M10). If you decide to leave, give notice and leave on good terms, and line up your next step first if you can.

🚀 Work for yourself

If the business slows down, look at what customers did buy, adjust, and consider a part-time job to steady your income while you rebuild.

🌍 Crossing borders

If plans change while you're studying abroad or in the US on a student visa, talk to your school's international office before you change anything. Your visa status may depend on it.
