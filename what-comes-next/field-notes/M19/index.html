Meet the Form Monster

Every path after graduation comes with paperwork: applications, forms, registrations, deadlines. Rocky calls it the Form Monster. It isn't evil. It's just hungry, it never sleeps, and it loves eating students who say "I'll do it later."

The good news: the Form Monster has one weakness. A calendar. Today you'll build one calendar that holds every deadline in every lane you're following, and you'll learn the trick that beats the monster every time: plan backward.

(Yes, this is Rocky's favorite module. He's been color-coding his excitement for weeks.)

2

Three kinds of dates

Not all dates are the same. Your calendar needs all three:

  1. Deadlines: the last possible day. Applications, registrations, priority aid dates.
  2. Do dates: the day you actually start. Work backward from the deadline: how long will it take, plus a cushion for surprises?
  3. Waiting-on-someone dates: anything that depends on another person. Recommendation letters, a parent's information for a financial aid form, a transcript, a school's reply.

That third kind is where most people get eaten. You can't control how fast other people move, so start the "waiting on someone" tasks first. Ask recommenders at least a month before the deadline. Ask your parents for paperwork early. Then do your own parts while you wait.

3

⚠️ Early-deadline paths

Some paths start long before senior year, and they surprise people every year:

  • Military service academies: you usually need a nomination, often from a member of Congress (the Coast Guard Academy is the exception: no nomination needed). Each office sets its own timeline, and many open applications in the spring of junior year and close them in the fall of senior year.
  • ROTC scholarships: applications often open the summer before senior year and close in winter or early spring. Each branch has its own dates.
  • College sports: homeschooled athletes who want to play at an NCAA Division I or II school need an NCAA Eligibility Center account plus extra homeschool paperwork from their parents. Start by sophomore or junior year.

If any of these interest you even a little, tell Rocky today. Finding out late is the one plot twist we really want to avoid.

4

Deadlines in every lane

🎓 College

  • Early Decision and Early Action deadlines are often in November. ⚠️ Early Decision is binding: you promise to attend if you're admitted. Early Action isn't. Check each school's rules and talk with your parents before you choose Early Decision. Regular Decision is usually in January or February. Some schools use rolling admission (they decide as applications arrive, so earlier is better).
  • Two more early flavors: Restrictive (or Single-Choice) Early Action isn't binding, but it limits where else you can apply early. Early Decision II is binding like ED, just with a later deadline. Read each school's early rules with a parent before you choose.
  • Application fees vary a lot: hundreds of colleges charge nothing, and many others charge somewhere around $50 to $90 (check each school). Fee waivers are available through the Common App and many colleges, and your parent, acting as your counselor, can confirm whether you qualify.
  • The FAFSA (the federal financial aid form) opens around late September or early October for the next school year. Many states and colleges have priority deadlines, and some aid runs out, so file early.
  • Some private colleges also want the CSS Profile, a second aid form.

🔧 Trade School / Apprenticeship

Trade programs often start at set times (fall, spring or several times a year), and popular ones fill up. Apprenticeship applications may open only once or twice a year, sometimes for just a few weeks, so watch for them and sign up for alerts.

💼 Career

Many summer internships take applications in winter. Job openings appear year-round, but some employers hire for summer and fall in big batches. For the military, enlistment involves several steps (talking with a recruiter, the ASVAB, a medical exam), so give yourself time.

🚀 Work for yourself

Set launch milestones: test your idea, register your business if needed (with a parent), set up a way to get paid, and plan a launch date. Game jams and markets have their own sign-up dates too.

🌍 Crossing borders

Visas and international applications can take months. Put those dates on the calendar first.

Build your calendar

Here's how to set it up:

  1. Pick one place for everything: a paper planner, a calendar app, or both. One. Not five.
  2. Add every deadline from your lanes, your testing plan (M18) and your goals (M15).
  3. Work backward from each deadline to set a do date, plus a cushion of a week or two.
  4. Mark the "waiting on someone" tasks and schedule them first.
  5. Set reminders a week before and a day before each big date.
  6. Check it every week. Sunday evening works great for a lot of people.

Two application habits: make a professional email address (something like firstname.lastname) and use it only for applications, so nothing important gets buried under game updates. After you apply, check each school's applicant portal weekly for "missing items", because one missing form can quietly hold up your whole file.

You'll build a full system to keep it all running in M26, the Command Center. For now, get every date in one place.

🏡 Homeschool Edge

Your flexible schedule is a superpower here. You can work on applications on a Tuesday morning, call offices while they're open, and visit schools when other students are in class. Just make sure flexible doesn't turn into "later." The monster loves "later."

👪 Ask Your Parents

  1. "Can we put my big deadlines on the family calendar too?"
  2. "When can you help with the parts of the FAFSA or other forms that need your information?"
  3. "Are there any family dates (trips, events) I should plan around?"
