1

Dreams, now with a to-do list

A dream says where you want to go. A goal plan says how you'll get there, one step at a time.

In M14 you chose your lanes, a first-choice path and a Plan B. Today you turn that path into a plan you can actually start this week. Not someday. Not "after I organize my desk." This week.

(Organizing your desk is allowed. It just doesn't count as a goal. Rocky checked the rules.)

2

Start far away

Great plans start at the end and work backward. So first, picture yourself five to ten years from now. Use your Daydream Tuesday from M04 and your first-choice path from M14:

  • What work are you doing?
  • Where are you living?
  • What are you proud of?
  • What does a normal day feel like?

This is your far goal, your big picture. It doesn't need to be perfect or permanent. It just needs to be exciting enough to pull you forward.

3

Backward planning

Now walk backward from the far goal, one step at a time:

  1. Five to ten years: the big picture.
  2. One year: what has to be true a year from now to stay on track?
  3. Three months: what has to happen this season to reach the one-year goal?
  4. This week: what's one small thing you can do in the next seven days?

Meet Priya. (Made up, but she has very organized sticky notes.) Priya wants to become a dental hygienist, a job that usually takes an associate degree from an accredited program and a state license.

  • Five years: working as a licensed dental hygienist in a busy family practice.
  • One year: accepted into an accredited dental hygiene program.
  • Three months: finish her chemistry course, shadow a hygienist, and make a list of three programs and what each one requires.
  • This week: with a parent's okay, email the dentist on her People Map (M08) to ask about shadowing.

See how the big dream turned into one email? That's the magic of backward planning. Big goals feel scary. One email feels doable.

4

Backward planning in every lane

Priya's path runs through 🎓 college. Here's how a first step looks on other trails:

  • 🔧 Electrician apprentice: far goal, a licensed journeyworker. This week: look up two apprenticeship programs near you on apprenticeship.gov and write down when each one takes applications.
  • 💼 Straight to work: far goal, managing a store. This week: make a list of three local employers known for training and promoting from within.
  • 🚀 Your own business: far goal, a photography business with regular clients. This week: offer a $0 test shoot to one family friend (with a parent's okay) and ask for honest feedback.
  • 🌍 Studying abroad: far goal, a degree in another country. This week: check whether your passport is current and find out how long a renewal takes.
5

Make your goals SMART

A fuzzy goal like "do better at science" is hard to act on. When do you start? How do you know when you're done? SMART goals fix that. A SMART goal is:

  • Specific: exactly what will you do?
  • Measurable: how will you know you did it?
  • Achievable: can you really do it with the time and help you have?
  • Relevant: does it move you toward your far goal?
  • Time-bound: by when?

Here's a goal makeover:

  • Before: "Get better at math."
  • After: "Finish chapters 5 through 8 of Algebra 2 by December 15, doing three lessons a week."

Same goal, but now it has a finish line. Finish lines are very motivating. (So are snacks. Rocky recommends both.)

6

Two kinds of goals

Some goals are about outcomes: getting accepted, landing the apprenticeship, earning a certificate. Outcomes matter, but you can't fully control them. Other people make some of those decisions.

Other goals are about process: the habits you can control. "Practice welding twice a week." "Work on applications every Sunday afternoon." "Send one networking email every Friday."

The best plans have both. You aim for the outcome, and you control the process. If an outcome doesn't go your way, your process goals still built real skills, and you'll be ready for the next door. (More on plot twists in M27.)

7

Goals are allowed to change

Your goals are a plan, not a promise carved into a mountain. As you learn more, you might adjust them. That's not failing. That's steering.

Rocky will check in on your goals in later modules. A good rhythm is to look at your three-month goal once a month and your this-week goal every week. In M19 you'll put every deadline on one calendar, and in M26 you'll build a system that keeps it all running.

8

🏡 Homeschool Edge

You've probably set your own learning goals and planned your own school days for years. That's real experience with planning, pacing and following through. Colleges, training programs and employers love people who can manage themselves, and you've been practicing.

9

🔭 Lane Peek

Take your far goal and imagine reaching it through a different lane. Could a 🔧 training program, a 💼 job that trains you, or a 🎓 degree get you to the same place? Many big goals have more than one road leading to them.

10

🍁 In Canada?

Trade licences and apprenticeships are run by your province. Swap "apprenticeship.gov" for your province's apprenticeship site (like ontario.ca's "Start an apprenticeship," SkilledTradesBC, Alberta's tradesecrets, or the CCQ in Quebec), and "state license" for "provincial licence." Your backward plan works exactly the same.

11

👪 Ask Your Parents

Share your plan with your parents and ask:

  1. "Here's my one-year goal. What do you think?"
  2. "What help might I need from you this year?" (Rides, time, money, paperwork, a quiet place to study.)
  3. "Can we check in on my goals together once a month?"

Rocky can help you turn your plan into a short note.
