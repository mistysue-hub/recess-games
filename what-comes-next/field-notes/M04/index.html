Start with the life, not the job title

So far you've found what pulls you in (M02) and what you're good at (M03). Now for the question most people forget to ask: what kind of life do you actually want?

A job isn't just the eight hours you spend at work. It decides where you live, when you wake up, how much free time you have, who you spend your days with, and what you can afford on the weekends. Two people with the exact same interests and strengths can want totally different lives, and both be right.

So today, instead of starting with "What job should I get?", we're starting with "What does a great Tuesday look like?" Then we'll work backward.

(Why Tuesday? Because nobody's life is all Saturdays. Tuesday is the real test.)

2

Your Daydream Tuesday at 25

Imagine it's an ordinary Tuesday, and you're 25 years old. Not a vacation day. A regular, normal, good day. Picture it in as much detail as you can:

  • Where do you wake up? A city apartment, a small town, a farmhouse, another country, a van with excellent Wi-Fi?
  • What time is it? (Be honest. Rocky won't judge your relationship with alarm clocks.)
  • Who's around? Roommates, a spouse, kids, a dog, your whole extended family down the street?
  • How do you get to work? Walk, drive, train, bike, or just roll over to your laptop?
  • Where do you work? Outside, in a workshop, an office, a hospital, a classroom, a kitchen, a studio, a truck, a different place every day?
  • What are you doing at 2 p.m.? Building, solving, helping, creating, leading, organizing?
  • Are you working alone or with a team? Quiet focus or busy and buzzing?
  • When does work end? At 5 p.m. sharp? Whenever the job's done? Does work follow you home?
  • What do you do in the evening? Hobbies, church, friends, family, sports, a side project, sleep?
  • What made today feel good?

You'll tell Rocky about your Tuesday in a minute. Notice which details you care about a lot, and which ones you don't care about at all. Both are useful.

3

What matters most to you at work

Your Tuesday points at your values: the things that make work feel worth it. Career experts usually group work values into six big ones:

  • 🏆 Achievement: using your skills and seeing results you're proud of.
  • 🧭 Independence: making your own decisions and working your own way. (🚀 People who want to be their own boss usually score high here.)
  • ⭐ Recognition: being respected, getting to lead, and moving up.
  • 🤝 Relationships: getting along with coworkers, and doing work that helps other people.
  • 🛡️ Support: a fair boss, good training, and a company that has your back.
  • 🏠 Working conditions: good pay, steady work, a schedule that fits your life, and variety.

Everybody wants a little of everything. The question is what you'd rank at the top when you can't have it all. (Spoiler: nobody gets it all. Even astronauts have paperwork.)

Take the Work Values Matcher. CareerOneStop's Work Values Matcher (careeronestop.org) is a free card sort from the US Department of Labor. You sort 20 statements about work by how important they are to you, and it shows your top values plus careers that match them. It takes about 10 minutes. Rocky will use your results later, when you weigh your options in M14, so this one really matters.

4

Lifestyle must-haves

Beyond values, think about your must-haves and deal-breakers:

  • 💵 Money: Do you want enough to live simply, or do you dream of a big house and lots of travel? (We'll get real numbers in M13. For now, just notice what you want.)
  • 📍 Location: Close to family? A big city? Somewhere specific? Anywhere with mountains? 🌍 Another country?
  • ⏰ Schedule: Regular 9-to-5? Flexible hours? Shifts? Long stretches on and then long stretches off? Summers free?
  • 👨‍👩‍👧 Family: Do you want time for a family of your own soon? Do you want to stay near the family you have?
  • 🌄 Adventure: Travel, new places, variety, a little risk?
  • 🙏 Faith and purpose: Does your work need to connect to something bigger than a paycheck?
  • 🚀 Being your own boss: Is that a must-have, a nice-to-have, or a "no thank you"?

There are no wrong answers here, just honest ones. And they can change. Lots of people want adventure at 20 and a short commute at 30.

5

How the lanes fit different lives

Different lanes tend to fit different lifestyles, at least at first:

  • 🎓 College or university often means a few more years of studying before full-time pay, and sometimes moving away. Some careers it leads to pay well and offer flexibility later on.
  • 🔧 Trade school or an apprenticeship usually means earning sooner (apprentices get paid while they learn). Many trades also lead to running your own crew or business someday.
  • 💼 Going straight into a career means income and independence right away, and learning on the job. Some paths, like the military, come with training, travel and benefits built in.
  • 🚀 Working for yourself brings the most freedom, and also the most responsibility. You're the boss, and also the person who has to do the taxes.

These are patterns, not rules. Plenty of people mix and match.

6

🔭 Lane Peek

Try this: look at your Daydream Tuesday and imagine reaching it through a lane you haven't been considering. Could a trade get you that flexible schedule? Could college get you to that city? Could a business let you work from that farmhouse? Sometimes a different road gets you to the exact same Tuesday.

7

🏡 Homeschool Edge

You may already know a lot about the kind of day that works for you. Do you love working at your own pace? Do you thrive with a schedule, or without one? Do you get your best work done at 7 a.m. or 10 p.m.? Homeschoolers often know this better than anyone. That self-knowledge will help you pick a life that fits. (We'll dig into how you work best in M05.)

8

👪 Ask Your Parents

This one makes for a great dinner conversation. Ask them:

  1. "What do you love most about your workday? What would you change?"
  2. "If you could give 25-year-old you one piece of advice about choosing a life, what would it be?"

Ask a Recess guide or mentor the same questions. You'll get very different answers, and that's the point: there's more than one good way to live.
