Mission control for your life

Here's a secret about successful people: they don't have better memories than you. They have better systems. A system catches the details so your brain can do the fun stuff, like learning, building, and figuring out what's for lunch.

As a homeschooler, you've already been managing your own time more than most teens. Today you'll turn that into a Command Center: one simple setup that keeps track of your calendar, your tasks, your people and your energy, and works even when other people (bosses, professors, clients) set the schedule. (This is Rocky's second-favorite module. The first is the calendar module. He'd like to note that this module also has a calendar.)

2

The four parts of your Command Center

  1. One calendar for anything with a time or date (you built it in M19).
  2. One task list for everything you need to do that doesn't have a set time.
  3. Reminders so nothing sneaks up on you.
  4. A weekly check-in to keep it all up to date.

Notice the word one. Five sticky notes, three apps and a mental list is not a system. It's a scavenger hunt.

3

Your task list

Write down every task as soon as you think of it: "email Coach Kim," "buy work boots," "finish FAFSA section 3." Your brain is for having ideas, not for storing them.

A good task starts with a verb and is small enough to start right away. "College stuff" isn't a task. "Look up Maple State's housing deadline" is.

4

Break it into 20-minute chunks

Big projects feel scary because they're big. So shrink them. Break any big task into chunks of about 20 minutes, then do just one chunk.

Example: "Write my scholarship essay" becomes:

  1. Read the prompt and brainstorm (20 min)
  2. Pick one story and outline it (20 min)
  3. Write a messy first half (20 min)
  4. Write a messy second half (20 min)
  5. Read it aloud and revise (20 min)

Starting is the hardest part. A 20-minute chunk is small enough that your brain stops arguing.

5

The weekly check-in

Once a week (Sunday evening works for lots of people), take 15 minutes to:

  1. Look back: what got done? Celebrate it.
  2. Clear the clutter: add any new tasks and dates.
  3. Look ahead: what's due in the next two weeks? What needs a "waiting on someone" ask now (M19)?
  4. Pick your top three for the week.
6

Keeping in touch with your people

Your network (M08) and your Cheer Squad (M22) are relationships, and relationships need care. Make a people list with each person and the last time you were in touch. Every month or two, send a short note: an update, a thank-you, or an article they'd like. Put "check people list" on your weekly check-in.

7

When someone else sets the schedule

Soon, a boss, a professor, a training program or a client will set some of your schedule. Some tips:

  • Put fixed times on your calendar first (classes, shifts, appointments), then fit your tasks around them.
  • Plan travel time and a buffer. Being 10 minutes early is the new on time.
  • Check your messages every day (email and work apps), and reply within a day.
  • If you'll be late or miss something, tell them before it happens, not after.
8

Your energy is part of the system

You're not a robot. Notice when you focus best (morning? evening?) and save your hardest chunks for then. Protect your sleep. Take breaks. Put your phone in another room during a focus chunk. These aren't extras. They're how the system keeps running.

9

Your Command Center in every lane

🎓 College

Put every syllabus date (tests, papers, office hours) on your calendar the first week of each term. Professors rarely send reminders.

🔧 Trade School / Apprenticeship

Track your class schedule, work shifts and the hours you log toward your apprenticeship. Keep copies of any hour records.

💼 Career

Track your shifts, training dates, pay days and any certification renewal dates. Keep a "win list" for your next review (M25).

🚀 Work for yourself

You're your own manager now. Schedule work blocks like appointments, track client deadlines and invoices, and set a "closing time" so work doesn't eat your whole life.

🌍 Crossing borders

Add visa and document renewal dates to your calendar with reminders well ahead. Missing one can cause big problems.
