Now showing: a plot twist

Here's a twist most people don't see coming: you don't have to wait until after graduation to take a college or university course. Some teens take a real course while they're still homeschooling. (Rocky has asked around for reviews. Five stars, mostly. One student said the online discussion board was "a lot.")

This isn't a decision for today. It's a preview, so that when the time comes, you know the door is there.

2

What is dual credit in Canada?

Dual credit means one course counts two times: as college credit and as high-school credit.

In Canada, provinces run dual-credit programs mainly for students enrolled in public high schools. Ontario's program, for example, is mostly for students close to earning their Ontario diploma, and the school principal makes the selection. BC and Alberta have school-district programs too.

So most homeschoolers aren't in these programs. But there are other doors, and they work a lot like dual credit: your parents count the course on your homeschool transcript, and the college or university gives you a real grade.

3

Homeschool doors to early courses

Every school has its own rules, and they change, so check with a parent:

  • Open universities: Athabasca University (Alberta, online) admits students 16 and older, and younger students only by special petition. Thompson Rivers University Open Learning (BC, online) has open admission for most courses, with no transcript needed for most of them. Check any age rules before you sign up.
  • Non-degree studies: some universities let people take a course without being in a degree. UBC Access Studies, for example, allows up to 6 credits a term (24 in total) and accepts some high-school students with a principal's recommendation and a parent's consent. Homeschoolers should email to ask.
  • College continuing-education courses: many colleges offer evening or online courses anyone can take.
  • Provincial high-school courses: not college courses, but they build an official mark from outside your home. Ontario's TVO ILC takes students 14 and older; BC has Distributed Learning schools.
4

Why students love it

  • It's proof. A college or university grade shows schools, programs and employers that you can do the work. For homeschoolers, this "outside proof" can really help with admissions.
  • It's a test drive. You find out what college or university feels like, with your family still right there to cheer you on.
  • It can save time. A course you finish now may count toward a program later.
  • It's for every lane. Colleges teach welding, early childhood education, coding, business and much more, not just English and math. A trades-curious student can get a head start too.
5

The fine print (every good trailer has some)

  • It usually isn't free. Some school-board dual-credit programs cost students little or nothing, but courses you take as a homeschooler usually have tuition and fees. Ask what you would pay.
  • Your grades stick. College and university grades start a real transcript that follows you. That's great when they're good. So start small: one course, not five.
  • Pick courses that travel well. Intro courses like English, psychology or calculus are more likely to transfer (count) at other schools. The school you apply to later decides, so check first. (The main course calls this the golden rule.)
  • Ask how it affects your application. Some universities treat courses taken before high-school graduation differently. With early credits, you'll usually still apply as a first-year student. Check each school's rule.
  • Sign-ups happen early. Some courses need paperwork weeks or months before they start.
  • You'll be in class with adults. Discussions can get grown-up, and instructors expect you to speak up for yourself. The school will usually talk to you, not your parents, about your grades.
6

When do people start?

Many students try their first course around ages 16 to 17, one course at a time, often the year after Sneak Peek. Your job right now is just to find out what's possible where you live, so you can make a plan with your family.

7

🏡 Homeschool Edge

You already know how to learn on your own, manage your time and talk to adults. Those are exactly the skills that make early-course students shine, especially in online courses where nobody reminds you. And your parents can fit a course right into your homeschool plan.

8

🌍 Heading south someday?

If you're thinking about US colleges, a US college grade from an online or community college course can be strong proof too. The US version of this module explains how American dual credit works.
