Now showing: the supporting cast

Every great movie has a supporting cast. The best friend. The wise mentor. The neighbor who somehow always shows up at the right moment.

You have a supporting cast too. It's bigger than you think. And most of the people in it would be happy to tell you about their work, if you ask.

Today you'll find out who's in your cast, and how to have a great conversation with them (with your parents' help).

2

Your People Map

Rocky is going to help you build a People Map. It's three simple rings:

  1. Inner ring: people you know well. Family, close friends, your Recess friends, guides and mentors.
  2. Middle ring: people you know a little. Co-op families, coaches, neighbors, family friends, your music teacher, the librarian who knows your name.
  3. Outer ring: people you'd like to meet someday, like someone who does a job you found on Job Safari. You don't know them yet, but someone in your inner ring might.

Type the names in your Field Notes on the page, with what each person does or knows about. When you copy your notes to Rocky, he'll keep the map in your profile. (He's already very excited. He has asked whether the rings can be color-coded by birthday month. The answer is no.)

The curious conversation

A curious conversation is a short chat, maybe 10 to 15 minutes, where you ask someone about their work. You're not asking for anything. You're just curious. Most grown-ups love it, because hardly anyone ever asks them about their job.

Here's how it works for you right now: a parent sets it up and is there, nearby, or on the call with you. That's the rule for any adult outside your family. Your parent might say, "My kid is curious about your work. Could they ask you a few questions?"

Good questions to ask:

  • "What does a normal day look like for you?"
  • "How did you get into this work?"
  • "What's the best part? What's the hardest part?"
  • "What did you study or train for, if anything?"
  • "If you were my age, what would you try?"

One question to skip: don't ask how much money they make. (It's fine to ask, "What do people usually start out earning in this kind of work?" if you're curious.)

Your cast can come from any lane. Talk to a nurse, an electrician, a store manager, someone who runs their own business. Every story teaches you something.

4

The thank-you note

Here's a tiny habit with a huge payoff: send a thank-you note within a day or two. It can be a card, an email or a text (sent with your parent's okay).

Keep it short:

"Thank you for talking with me about your work! I loved hearing about _____. Now I want to learn more about _____."

That's all. People remember the teen who said thank you. Sometimes for years.

5

Your Recess crew

Don't forget the people already cheering for you on Recess. Your friends, guides and mentors are part of your inner ring. Ask them, "How did you figure out what to do?" Some of them took winding roads, and their stories might surprise you. Rocky can help you think of good questions.

6

🏡 Homeschool Edge

Homeschoolers often have a wide, mixed-age cast: co-op parents, coaches, neighbors, librarians and the adults you meet on field trips. You're probably used to chatting with grown-ups, which makes curious conversations much easier. That's a real head start.
