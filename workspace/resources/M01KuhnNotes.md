# M01 notes (Rocky only — never open on screen or read out)

## Hook answer
False. Scheele made the gas first (around 1771–72), Priestley made it and published first (1774–75), and Lavoisier first understood it as an element.

## Talk-back (one at a time, after the look-back)
1. Priestley and Lavoisier looked at the same gas. What did each of them think it was?
   Answer: Priestley thought it was air with its phlogiston removed ("dephlogisticated air"). Lavoisier thought it was a new element, which he named oxygen.
2. Why might a science book tell the tidy version instead of the messy one?
   Any thoughtful answer counts. The reading's answer: books teach today's ideas fast and don't have room for every wrong turn.

## Side View question
Core: Think of LEGO bricks. Bacon pictured science as a pile you keep adding to. Kuhn said sometimes the pile gets knocked over and rebuilt a new way. Which fits the oxygen story better, and why?
Extended: Bacon pictured science as a pile of facts that keeps growing. Kuhn said the pile sometimes gets knocked over and rebuilt. Which picture fits the oxygen story better, and why?
Opinion question: no right answer. Stay neutral.

## Game: Timeline Tidy-Up
Rounds: put the oxygen events in order by tapping (Core 4 events, Extended 6), then judge four history sentences as "Too tidy" or "Fair." Extended adds a round sorting four sentences into "Whig history" or "Fair history." Wrong answers show an explanation on screen. The last screen is "Notebook closed" with scores and a Copy button.
Stuck nudge: "Check the dates in your reading." Never give the order.

## Activity 2 (only if the activity count is two)
Ask the learner to name something they learned as "one person discovered it" (any subject). Then ask what the messier story might be. Accept any thoughtful guess and say it's a guess, not a fact.

## Anomaly Log
Tell them to start their Anomaly Log now, in the box at the bottom of the reading page or on paper. Ask: "Did anything in this module seem too tidy or left out? Write it down, then tell me one thing you wrote." The reading's own Anomaly line (shown only after they tap the reveal button) is a fine answer too, and so is "nothing this time."
