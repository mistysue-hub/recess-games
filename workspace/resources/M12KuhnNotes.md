# M12 notes (Rocky only — never open on screen or read out)

## Hook answer
Opinion: no single right answer. The look-back: the reading starts from Kuhn's own change of mind, then asks the learner to test his pattern on their own. Later it says a two-line map squashes ideas into boxes, so it's a small paradigm too. The hook gives examples (a rule, a fact, a game strategy), so a small, light example is fine.

## Talk-back (one at a time, after the look-back)
1. Tell me about a belief you changed. What didn't fit, and what made you switch?
   Their own story. Keep it light and age-appropriate; if they'd rather not share something personal, they can pick an example about a game, a subject or how something works. Never push for private details.
2. Did your story fit Kuhn's pattern, or not? Where didn't it fit?
   Any honest answer. A misfit is a good answer.

## Side View question
Core: Pick one thinker on the map to have on your side in an argument about screen time. Who would you pick, and why? (Philosophers today still don't agree on who was right.)
Extended: Suppose a surprising science claim shows up in the news tomorrow. Which thinker on the map would you trust most to help you decide whether to believe it, and why?
Opinion question: no right answer. Stay neutral.

## Game: Map the Thinkers
https://mistysue-hub.github.io/recess-games/paradigm-shift/map-the-thinkers/?level=core (or ?level=extended)
Backup: https://raw.githack.com/mistysue-hub/recess-games/main/paradigm-shift/map-the-thinkers/index.html?level=core
The learner places each thinker on a 3-by-3 map by tapping a square (trust: rules ↔ community; aim: truth ↔ usefulness), then places themselves. Core has four thinkers (Popper, Kuhn, Feyerabend, Longino); Extended has five (the same four plus Lakatos). After each, the game shows one common reading of where the thinker sits and says thinkers resist boxes. Placements are the learner's judgment; there is no score for matching. The last screen is "Notebook closed" with their map.
