# M06 notes (Rocky only — never open on screen or read out)

## Hook answer
False. Copernicus's 1543 model was not more accurate than Ptolemy's, and it did not use fewer circles overall. Kepler's ellipses (1609), fitted to Tycho Brahe's data, made the big jump in accuracy.

## Talk-back (one at a time, after the look-back)
1. If Copernicus wasn't more accurate, what did his Sun-centered model explain better?
   Answer: the backward loops (retrograde motion). They happen when Earth passes an outer planet, so no extra circle is needed for that.
2. Why did Kepler throw out a model that missed by only about an eighth of a degree?
   Answer: he trusted Tycho Brahe's measurements, which were more precise than that, more than he trusted his own model.

## Side View question
Core: Think of two ways to fix a bad video-game strategy: a patch that only fixes yesterday's loss, or a new idea that also wins games you haven't played yet. Lakatos called the first kind "degenerating" and the second "progressive," but don't use those words with a Core learner. Which was Kepler's ellipse? Why?
Extended: Lakatos said a "progressive" theory keeps predicting new things; a "degenerating" one only patches. Was Kepler's ellipse progressive or degenerating? Why?
Opinion question, but most people say progressive because it fit Tycho's data and led to new laws. Stay neutral.

## Facts to get right
- Never say Ptolemy's followers piled "epicycles on epicycles." That's a myth. Never say Copernicus used fewer circles or was more accurate.
- Ptolemy wrote around the year 150 in Alexandria. Copernicus published in 1543. Kepler published his first two laws in 1609.

## Activity: Epicycle Builder (both levels)
The learner sees 13 careful observations of Mars over one year (dots on a sky strip, with a backward loop). They add Ptolemy's tools one at a time, predicting first whether each helps. Extended: one big circle, epicycle, eccentric, equant, and each step shows the average miss in degrees and how many dots match within a tenth of a degree. Core: only one big circle, epicycle and "Ptolemy's finishing tweaks" (the eccentric and equant are not named), and each step shows only "dots matched, N of 13." Then they try Copernicus's model (same matches as Ptolemy's best) and Kepler's ellipse (all 13 match). The last screen is "Notebook closed."
Stuck nudge: "Tap the next tool and see what happens to the dots." Never give the scores.

## Activity 2 (Core: only if the activity count is two; Extended: runs by default), Extended only
Open the Foothill Ptolemaic simulator: https://foothillastrosims.github.io/Ptolemaic-System-Simulator/ . Ask them to choose the Mars preset, tap Start Animation, and watch. Ask: "When Mars moves backward, where is it on its small circle: the side near Earth or the side away from Earth?" Answer: the side nearest Earth.
Core learners: instead ask them to describe passing a slower car or runner, and whether the slower one ever seemed to go backward.
