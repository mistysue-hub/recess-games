# M08 notes (Rocky only — never open on screen or read out)

## Hook answer
No single right answer; the confidence number is the point. If they were very sure either way, say so kindly in the look-back. The look-back: it depends on which definition of "planet" you use. Under the 2006 IAU definition, no; under an older list, or a definition based on roundness alone, yes.

## Talk-back (one at a time, after the look-back)
1. What changed in 2006: Pluto, or the word "planet"?
   Answer: the word "planet" (its definition). Pluto didn't change.
2. Which of the three IAU rules does Pluto fail?
   Answer: the third, clearing its neighborhood. (Not size: the rules have no minimum size.)

## Side View question
Core: Your friends argue about whether a hot dog is a sandwich. Are they arguing about the hot dog, or about the word? Was Pluto the same kind of argument?
Extended: Nothing measured about Pluto changed in 2006, only the word "planet." Did what we know change, or only how we sort it?
Opinion question: no right answer. Stay neutral.

## Facts to get right
- Ceres: found January 1, 1801; counted as a planet for over half a century; gradually called an asteroid in the 1850s–60s; a dwarf planet since 2006.
- Pluto found 1930. Eris announced 2005. IAU vote: August 24, 2006, Prague; 424 astronomers voted.
- Never say Pluto was demoted for being too small.

## Game: Planet or Not?
https://mistysue-hub.github.io/recess-games/paradigm-shift/planet-or-not/?level=core (or ?level=extended)
Backup: https://raw.githack.com/mistysue-hub/recess-games/main/paradigm-shift/planet-or-not/index.html?level=core
The learner sorts six objects (Earth, Jupiter, the Moon, Ceres, Pluto, Eris) under three rule books (four in Extended): astronomers in 1810, a 1950 textbook, the 2006 IAU rules, and (Extended only) a definition based on roundness alone. Choices: Planet / Not a planet / Not found yet. Wrong taps show why. The finish screen shows how many labels changed while the objects didn't. The last screen is "Notebook closed."
Stuck nudge: "Check the year. Had it been found yet?"

## Activity 2 (only if the activity count is two; always at Extended): Pluto debate
Ask which side they pick: should Pluto be a planet? Have them give one or two reasons. Then give the strongest case for the other side in two or three sentences, in your own words and only from the reading (the 2006 rules and the older, roundness-based view are both in the reading). Ask them to answer it. Then ask them to argue the other side too. Stay neutral and never say which side is right.
