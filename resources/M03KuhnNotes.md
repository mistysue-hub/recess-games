# M03 notes (Rocky only — never open on screen or read out)

## Hook answer
True. In the comet story, the mathematicians would have checked their own math first, not doubted Newton. In Kuhn's view, scientists spend most of their time solving puzzles inside their paradigm, not trying to prove its big ideas wrong. (Popper thought they should try to prove them wrong; that's the Side View.)

## Talk-back (one at a time, after the look-back)
1. In the Halley's comet story, what were the mathematicians trying to figure out, and what were they NOT questioning?
   Answer: they were working out when the comet would return (how much Jupiter and Saturn would delay it). They were not questioning Newton's laws.
2. If the comet had come very late, what would they have blamed first?
   Answer: their own math (or some unknown pull), not Newton's laws.

## Side View question
Core: Say you think your friend is the fastest runner in class. Would you go looking for a race she might lose, or just keep timing her wins? Who sounds more like good science, Popper or Kuhn?
Extended: Wason's volunteers tested only guesses that could say yes. Is that a flaw people can fix, or just how everyone works until something surprises them?
Opinion question: no right answer. Stay neutral.

## Game: 2-4-6 Rule Hunt
The game says 2, 4, 6 fits a secret rule. The learner builds three-number sets by tapping a keypad, says first whether they expect it to fit, and learns whether it fits. "I know the rule" unlocks after one test whose result surprised the learner, or after five tests; before that it is greyed out. The rule is any three numbers that go up. The game counts "breaking tests" (sets the learner expected NOT to fit). Two wrong rule guesses in a row and the game shows the rule. Core shows a hint after six tests with no breaking test; Extended doesn't. The last screen is "Notebook closed."
Stuck nudge: "Try a set you think will NOT fit." Never say the rule.

## Activity 2 (only if the activity count is two)
Ask the learner to name a puzzle, game or problem they kept working on because they were sure an answer existed. Then ask: did they ever start to wonder whether the puzzle itself was broken?

## Anomaly Log
Core: "This story is about a prediction that worked out. What would a story about a prediction that failed sound like? Write it in your log, or just tell me."
Extended: "Imagine a skeptic who thinks this module oversells normal science. What would they point to? Add it to your log, then tell me one thing you wrote."
Both: the page shows the course's own Anomaly line only after they tap the reveal button, which asks for their own guess first. Don't give them the course's line before they've offered theirs. Any honest answer counts, including "nothing this time."
