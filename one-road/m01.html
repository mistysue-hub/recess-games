One Road · Day 1

The Boy in the Felt Tent

c. 1162 to 1206 · How an abandoned boy became Chinggis Khan

Before you ride

Almost everything we know about Temüjin's childhood comes from one book, the Secret History of the Mongols. Mongols wrote it for the royal family after he died. It's the family's own story of its founder. As you read, ask: what might they want you to believe?
WHO'S WHO · rough sounds
  • Temüjin (TEM-oo-jin): the boy. Later Chinggis Khan (CHING-iss).
  • Hö'elün (HUR-eh-loon): his mother. Börte (BUR-teh): his wife.
  • Bo'orchu (BOH-or-choo): first true follower. Jamuqa (JAH-moo-kah): his sworn brother, later a rival.

Other names are only here once. You don't need to memorize them.

PART 1

A family left behind

Temüjin was born around 1162 on the Onon River, in a world of felt tents, horses and herds that moved with the grass. When he was about nine, his father, Yesügei, took him to find a bride. Temüjin was promised to a girl named Börte and stayed with her family.

On the ride home, Yesügei shared a meal with some Tatars, old enemies, and fell ill. He died soon after. The Secret History says he was poisoned.

His clan didn't stay loyal to a widow with small children. They packed up the herds and left. Temüjin's mother, Hö'elün, kept her children alive on roots, wild fruit and fish.

PART 2

A killing in the family

Hunger made the brothers fight. When their half-brothers Bekter and Belgütei snatched a fish from Temüjin and Qasar, Hö'elün scolded them all. Their own clan had deserted them, she said, and enemies were everywhere:

We have no friend but our shadow,
We have no whip but our horse's tail.Secret History §76

Temüjin and Qasar ignored her. They crept up on Bekter and shot him with arrows. When they came home, their mother's first words were:

You who have destroyed life!Secret History §78
PART 3

The wooden collar

The clan that had abandoned them, the Tayichi'ud, came back and captured Temüjin. They locked his neck in a heavy wooden collar called a cangue. During a feast he knocked his guard down and ran, collar and all, and hid in the river.

A man named Sorqan Shira saw him and didn't give him away. His family took off the collar, burned it, and hid Temüjin in a cart piled with wool while searchers poked through the camp. They were sheltering their own masters' enemy.

PART 4

Followers you choose

When thieves stole the family's horses, a young stranger named Bo'orchu rode out with Temüjin to get them back and asked for nothing. He became Temüjin's first true follower.

Temüjin married Börte. When the Merkit tribe raided and carried her off, he rescued her with two allies: his sworn brother Jamuqa and Toghrul, a powerful khan who had been his father's sworn friend. Followers like these weren't kin. They chose him, and he rewarded loyalty, not birth.

PART 5

One banner

Years earlier, his own followers had sworn loyalty and given him the name Chinggis (§123). Over time he beat or absorbed every rival, including Jamuqa and Toghrul. Then in 1206 all the peoples of the felt tents gathered at the source of the Onon River.

They hoisted the white standard with nine tailsSecret History §202

and made Chinggis Khan ruler of them all.

The record says they could. By 1206 a herding people with no cities had united the steppe, and you just saw how: followers who chose him, and loyalty rewarded over birth.
