Two Friars at the Khan's Court

1245 to 1255 · Envoys, a silver tree and a debate between faiths

Before you ride

Two friars from Europe walked into the Mongol court and wrote down what they saw. Today you read them like a detective: which of their claims can we check, and which do we have to take on their word?

PART 1

A silver tree that poured four drinks

In the spring of 1254 a Flemish friar named William of Rubruck reached Karakorum, the Mongol capital. Outside the great khan Möngke's palace he found something no one in Europe would believe: a silver tree that poured drinks. A Paris goldsmith, Guillaume Boucher, had built it after ending up at the Mongol court. William described its base:

at its roots are four lions of silver, each with a conduit through it, and all belching forth white milk of mares.William of Rubruck
IN PLAIN WORDS Four silver lions at the base poured out mare's milk, a drink the Mongols loved.

Pipes at the top poured other drinks into silver basins. At the top stood an angel holding a trumpet, and underneath the tree a vault where a man could hide, ready to blow a pipe when the khan wanted a drink. Nothing of the tree survives; we have only William's word.

PART 2

Friar John goes first, 1245 to 1247

Ten years earlier, Pope Innocent IV sent another friar, John of Plano Carpini, to find out who these conquerors were. John arrived in the summer of 1246, just in time to see a new great khan, Güyük, raised to the throne. He wrote that there were

more than four thousand envoysJohn of Plano Carpini

at the ceremony, bringing gifts.

John carried home Güyük's answer to the Pope. It didn't offer friendship. It told the Pope to come in person, with his kings, and submit to the khan. That letter, in Persian with the khan's seal, was found in the Vatican archives in 1920.

PART 3

A city of quarters

William of Rubruck described Karakorum as a city where the empire met:

There are two quarters in it; one of the Saracens in which are the marketsWilliam of Rubruck
IN PLAIN WORDS One quarter was for Muslims, and that is where the markets were.

The other, he wrote, was "the quarter of the Cathayans, all of whom are artisans." ("Saracens" meant Muslims, "Cathayans" meant people from northern China.) He counted twelve temples of different peoples, two mosques and one church, and sniffed that the monastery of Saint-Denis back home was "ten times larger than the palace."

Can we check him? Since 1999 a Mongolian-German team has dug in the center of Karakorum and found a craftsmen's quarter: workshops for metal, glass, gemstones and bone, full of Chinese coins. It matches William's quarter of the Cathayans. No one has dug in the Muslim quarter yet.

PART 4

The great debate

In May 1254, on the eve of the Christian holy day of Pentecost, Möngke ordered a debate between the faiths at his court: Christians (William among them), Muslims, and Buddhists, whom William called "Tuins." The khan made it look fair:

Mangu Chan sent three secretaries who were to be umpires, one a Christian, one a Saracen, and one a TuinWilliam of Rubruck
IN PLAIN WORDS Möngke named three judges: a Christian, a Muslim and a Buddhist.

They argued at length. Nobody converted. At the end, William wrote, "they all drank deeply." Later Möngke explained his view to William: the Mongols believed in one God, "But as God gives us the different fingers of the hand, so he gives to men divers ways."

Watch the witness. William was no neutral reporter. He thought the Eastern Christians at court, the Nestorians, were heretics, and he described their priests as "getting drunk all day at court." When a witness dislikes someone, look for a second source.

Look back at your vote

You voted on: "A ruler who lets every religion practice freely must be tolerant."

The real answer: not necessarily. Möngke welcomed every faith and even staged a debate between them. Yet his cousin Güyük, just eight years earlier, ordered the Pope to come and submit. Mongol khans could be open about religion and still demand total political obedience. Openness to faiths and tolerance of rivals aren't the same thing.
