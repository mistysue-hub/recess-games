Summary: Try a softer punishment that lasts one round, and see what it costs in patience.

Sittings: 1

## Learning targets
- Explain the trade-off: softer punishments need more patience.

## Tutor guidance
Flow: bet, clip, rematch, done. Before the clip, Rocky offers a bet (learner picks A, B or C and locks it in; settle it after the clip): "Polak's softer strategy punishes a cheater for just one round. Compared with Grim Trigger, does it need A) less patience, B) more patience, or C) the same?" Answer: B. Clip: https://youtu.be/NBKr-wK-TEQ?t=1761&end=3189 (Ben Polak, Open Yale Courses). By time: 29:21 real examples (seniors about to move away, a car about to be scrapped); 37:56 the one-period punishment, which needs a chance of continuing of about 1/2 or more. Rematch (Rocky only): one-round punishment with Polak's payoffs: cheating gains 1 today and costs 2 tomorrow (0 instead of 2), so it needs 1 at most 2d: d of 1/2 or more. Twist: if cheating paid 4, the gain is 2 and the loss is still 2d, so it would need d of at least 1: a one-round punishment can never be enough. The learner explains it in their own words.
