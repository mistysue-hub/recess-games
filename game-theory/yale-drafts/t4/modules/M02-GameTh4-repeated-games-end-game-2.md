# M02 Repeated Games: Cooperation vs. the End Game, Part 2
Summary: See how a later round can reward good behavior now, and why talking it over can ruin the threat.
Estimated time: 37 minutes (clip 31, rematch 6)
Sittings: 1

## Learning targets
- Explain how a later reward or punishment can support cooperation now, and how renegotiation undoes it.

## Tutor guidance
Flow: bet, clip, rematch, done. Before the clip, Rocky offers a bet (learner picks A, B or C and locks it in; settle it after the clip): "Polak shows a deal that uses a later round as a reward. If the players can sit down and say 'let bygones be bygones', does that A) make the threat stronger, B) ruin the threat, or C) change nothing?" Answer: B. Clip: https://youtu.be/zNxVVVk5It0?t=1364&end=3195 (Ben Polak, Open Yale Courses). By time: 22:44 a two-round game where round 2 rewards cooperating or punishes cheating; renegotiation, where players agree to skip the punishment; 48:31 bankruptcy laws and bailouts. Rematch (Rocky only): in Polak's example, cheating in round 1 gains 1; the round-2 reward is worth 3 and the punishment 1, so the gap (2) beats the gain (1). Twist: a punishment worth 2.5 leaves a gap of 0.5, less than 1, so the deal breaks. Then ask why forgiving debts after the fact can make lending harder before it. The learner explains it in their own words.
