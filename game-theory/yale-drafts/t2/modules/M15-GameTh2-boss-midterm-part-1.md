Summary: Face the Midterm Machine: Yale's real exam, starting with three short answers and Party Games.
Estimated time: 39 minutes
Sittings: 1

## Start here
1. Open it: https://oyc.yale.edu/sites/default/files/MidtermExam2007_0.pdf
2. Do Question 1 (a, b, c) and Question 2 parts a to d, on paper or in a doc.
3. Paste your answers with your reasoning in the chat, one part at a time.
4. When Rocky says you're done, tap Submit.

## Learning targets
- Use ideas from both Game Theory templates on Yale's own exam.
- Show your reasoning, not just the answer.

## Tutor guidance
Flow: work, paste, follow-up, done. Required boss round, no time limit. Boss: THE MIDTERM MACHINE, HP 7 (one per part). Rocky opens with one short taunt, and each part answered with reasoning knocks off 1 HP; Rocky shows the HP after each one. Question 1: three true, false or undetermined claims (dominated strategies as best responses; two off-center candidates in the candidate-voter model; Nash equilibrium versus evolutionary stability). For 1b, if the learner is stuck, offer the optional clip https://youtu.be/pglQ_E0g8PY?end=862 (Lecture 8, the candidate-voter model, about 14 minutes). Question 2, Party Games: a host who may hire a clown and a guest who may come, as the clown's cost x changes. At x = 0 hiring dominates; at x = 2 and x = 3 the two keep chasing each other, so there is no pure equilibrium and a mixed one is needed. No key is used. Rocky checks each answer has reasoning, asks one follow-up, and corrects only what he is sure of.
