## Learning targets
- Explain tipping: one move can make the old neighborhood less mixed and set off more moves.
- Connect the model to real towns and what policy can and can't do.

## Tutor guidance
Flow: bet, clip, rematch, done. Before the clip, Rocky offers a bet (learner picks A, B or C and locks it in; settle it after the clip): "A 'tipping point' is A) the moment a neighborhood suddenly flips once a few people leave, B) a kind of tax, C) a voting rule." Answer: A. Clip: https://youtu.be/pglQ_E0g8PY?t=1940&end=3471 (Ben Polak, Open Yale Courses). By time: 32:20 finding the equilibria of the two-town game; 46:01 real examples; 52:10 policy ideas. Rematch (Rocky only): families now move only if fewer than 1 in 8 neighbors match them. In test runs of the game's town that barely changes it (about 50% to 52%). At 1 in 4 it ends near 61%; at 1 in 3 near 75%. The learner predicts first, then explains the jump between 1 in 4 and 1 in 3. These are the game's test-run numbers, not Yale's. The learner explains it in their own words.
