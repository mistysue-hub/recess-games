## TEMPLATE A: Beginner strategic thinking

### A1. What makes a game (players, moves, payoffs)
- **Video:** Game Theory 101 (#1): Introduction. William Spaniel. `NSVmOC_5zrE` [V]. About 5-7 min (unconfirmed). College-style whiteboard lecture that is clear and clean but dry, so best for ages 14+.
- **Video (alternative):** SciShow `MHS-htjGgSY` (see the cross-cutting table).
- **Activity:** Wharton Global Youth, "Game Theory: Strategies and Payoffs" (Grades 9-12). https://globalyouth.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/lesson_plans/game-theory.pdf [V]
  - The lesson uses the four parts of a game (players, strategies, rules, payoffs), a graffiti/school-suspension version of the PD, and a Coke vs. Pepsi pricing example.
  - Solo with a tutor: the tutor plays the second "artist." The learner labels players, strategies and payoffs for 3 everyday situations, such as group projects or which video game to buy.
- **Activity:** Cornell Math Explorers' Club, "Game Theory" (6 lessons for "mathematically interested high school students," with two problem sets per lesson and solutions). https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~mec/2008-2009/Anema/games.html [V]
  - Topics are Nash equilibria and mixed strategies, strategic moves, cooperation and sequential decisions. Good as a tutor-led worksheet bank for ages 14-16.

### A2. Dominant strategies
- **Video:** Game Theory 101 (#2): The Prisoner's Dilemma and Strict Dominance. Spaniel. `DanTKx1FLY8` [V]. Short lecture (unconfirmed length). Best for ages 14+.
- **Activity:** Have the learner fill in a 2x2 table and cross out dominated rows. The Cornell MEC problem sets work for this.

### A3. Prisoner's Dilemma
- **Top video:** TED-Ed, Husted `emyi4z-O0ls` [V], 5:24.
- **Hook video:** Golden Balls "Split or Steal" (Nick vs. Ibrahim). `S0qjK3TWZE8` [V], uploaded by "spinout3" as an **unofficial upload of an ITV clip**. Length is unconfirmed, about 5-7 min. It may be taken down, so have a backup. Content is clean, apart from tense arguing about lying.
  - Companion audio: Radiolab, "The Golden Rule." https://radiolab.org/podcast/golden-rule-2304 (the transcript is at /transcript).
- **Interactive:** Per Thirty Six, Prisoner's Dilemma Simulator. https://perthirtysix.com/tool/prisoners-dilemma-simulator [V]
  - Play single rounds against Random or Tit-for-Tat, then run head-to-head strategies and a population simulation with a noise slider.
  - No signup or ads seen, only newsletter and "buy me a coffee" links. It has a responsive viewport tag, but tablet use and embedding are unconfirmed.
- **Activity:** Athena Aktipis, "Cooperation Theory for the Classroom." https://www.athenaaktipis.org/blog/cooperation-theory-for-the-classroom [V]
  - Three PD games: assigned strategies, then free choice, then repeated play with "walk away" allowed. Uses an "Apple Tree" framing for younger students, with free PDFs for cards and score sheets.
  - Solo with a tutor: the tutor plays three personas, one per game, and the learner tracks scores on the provided sheet.
- **Activity:** Wharton "Keep or Share" quiz-points game (same PDF as A1).
  - Played once, then repeated an unannounced number of times. With a tutor, play one round, then a repeated round, and compare.

### A4. Best response and Nash equilibrium
- **Video:** Game Theory 101 (#4): Pure Strategy Nash Equilibrium and the Stag Hunt. Spaniel. `stzPcqmyhI4` [V]. Ages 14+.
- **Video:** Khan Academy, "Prisoners' dilemma and Nash equilibrium" (Sal Khan, microeconomics unit). https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics/imperfect-competition/oligopoly-and-game-theory/v/prisoners-dilemma-and-nash-equilibrium. The page exists, but the title and length were not confirmed because the page renders client-side.
- **Activity:** Cornell MATH 1300 activity overview. https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~activelearn/1300Materials/ActivityOverviews/GameTheory.pdf [V]
  - Students find the Nash equilibria of the PD and Bach-or-Stravinsky, play Golden Balls for candy and run a die-driven iterated tournament.
  - It is college level, but the candy/Golden Balls parts adapt easily: the tutor plays the opponent.
  - **Flag:** it suggests a clip from *A Beautiful Mind* (rated PG-13), so skip the clip or preview it.

### A5. Coordination games
- **Video:** Spaniel's Stag Hunt video (A4).
- **Activity (tutor-led):** a "Meet in the city with no phone" game, a Schelling focal-point exercise. The learner and tutor each write a place and time privately, then reveal. No external source is needed; Schelling's focal points are explained in his Nobel material (see C3).

### A6. Guessing games (two-thirds of the average, level-k)
- **Top video:** Game theory challenge: Can you predict human behavior? (Lucas Husted). TED-Ed. `MknV3t5QbUc` [V]. 4:40 on ted.com, 4:59 as an ed.ted lesson. Safe.
- **Real data hook:** Wait But Why reader contest. https://waitbutwhy.com/table/can-you-guess-what-other-readers-are-guessing-results [V]
  - 4,940 valid guesses, an average of about 22.96, and a winning number of 15.
  - Mild flag: it mentions that crude-word entries were thrown out, but none are printed.
- **Classroom background:** UBC Gamelab, "Guess two thirds" (40 players, winning guess 16). https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~hauert/research/gamelab/twothirds.html [V]. This is an old static page with results only, not playable.
- **Teaching notes:** Vince Knight, "Using the two thirds of the average game in class." https://vknight.org/unpeudemath/pedagogy/2015/07/23/Using-the-two-thirds-of-the-average-game-in-class.html (found in search, not fetched)
- **Solo with a tutor:** the learner guesses, then the tutor reveals real crowd data (WBW: 15; UBC: 16) and walks through level-0, level-1 and level-2 reasoning.

### A7. Location games (Hotelling, median voter)
- **Top video:** Why do competitors open their stores next to one another? (Jac de Haan). TED-Ed. `jILgxeNBK_8` [V]. 4:07. Uses ice-cream vendors on a beach. Safe.
- **Interactive:** Nicky Case, *To Build a Better Ballot*. https://ncase.me/ballot/ [NF]
  - An explorable on voting systems and the spoiler effect, about 20-30 min.
  - Not fetched this session. Nicky Case's sites are public domain/CC0, HTML5, free of ads and signup, and generally tablet-friendly. Embedding is likely to work (static hosting), but test it.
- **Real-world hook:** gas stations and fast-food clusters, and candidates moving to the center (a TED-Ed lesson extension).

### A8. Mixed strategies (rock-paper-scissors, penalty kicks)
- **Top video:** Winning at Rock Paper Scissors. Numberphile (Hannah Fry). `rudzYPHuewc` [V][V-len]. 5:47.
  - Covers psychological patterns and strategies; it is also an ed.ted "Best of Web" lesson. Safe.
- **Video:** Simulating the Evolution of Rock, Paper, Scissors. Primer. `tCoEYFbDVoI` [V][V-len]. 15:00, so it is long.
  - Cute blob creatures. Use roughly the first 6-8 minutes, or move it to Template C.
- **Video:** Spaniel's Rock Paper Scissors lesson. https://gametheory101.com/courses/game-theory-101/rock-paper-scissors/ (ID not extracted).
- **Activity:** SERC Carleton, "Penalty Kicks: A Simultaneous Move Zero-Sum Game." https://serc.carleton.edu/econ/experiments/examples/71339.html [V]
  - Kicker vs. goalie, each choosing from five directions with face-down cards, plus a "scout" who looks for patterns. 15-30 rounds, about 15-20 min. College level, but the mechanics are simple.
  - Solo with a tutor: the tutor plays goalie with a deliberate bias, and the learner scouts and exploits it. Then they swap roles, and the learner tries to be unpredictable.
- **Hooks:**
  - Real RPS behavior study (Zhejiang Univ., 2014): MIT Technology Review, "How to win at rock-paper-scissors." https://www.technologyreview.com/2014/04/30/13423/how-to-win-at-rock-paper-scissors/
  - Penalty-kick research: Palacios-Huerta, "Professionals Play Minimax." https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/26561/
  - Jens Lehmann's penalty crib-sheet note (Germany vs. Argentina, 2006 World Cup): FIFA. https://inside.fifa.com/news/the-piece-of-paper-that-helped-germany-turn-the-page-2811265

### A9. Price wars
- **Top video:** Crash Course Economics #26, `PCcVODWm-oY` [V]. Covers Coke/Pepsi-style competition and cartels.
- **Activity:** the Wharton Coke vs. Pepsi pricing matrix (A1 PDF).
  - Solo with a tutor: they play 5 rounds of "High/Low price" with the tutor as the rival store, first one-shot, then repeated.

## TEMPLATE B: Sequential games

### B1. Backward induction
- **Top video:** Working backward to solve problems (Maurice Ashley, chess grandmaster). TED-Ed / TEDYouth. `v34NqCbAA1c` [V]. About 5:57. Safe and inspiring.
- **Video:** Game Theory 101 (#17): Backward Induction. Spaniel. `pyLKkN5HpDY` [V][V-len]. 4:49. Ages 14+.
- **Activity:** SERC Carleton, "Introduction of Backward Induction Technique Using a Classroom Experiment" (Game of 21). https://serc.carleton.edu/econ/experiments/examples/68989.html [V]
  - Players take 1-3 numbers from 1-21 in turn, and whoever takes 21 wins. Replay on boards of 17, 13, 9 and 5 numbers, then reason backward. About 15-20 min, with no materials needed.
  - **Ideal solo:** the learner plays the tutor, and the tutor always wins until the learner cracks the "multiples of 4" pattern.

### B2. Nim / race-to-21 take-away games
- **Interactive:** NRICH, "Got It" (reach the target of 23 by adding 1-4; a Nim variant; ages 7-14; play against the computer or a friend).
  - Problem page: https://nrich.maths.org/1272 [V]
  - Game: https://nrich.maths.org/gotit2/ (linked from the problem page and also offered as a "Tablet/Full Screen Version")
  - No signup. NRICH (University of Cambridge) is ad-free. Likely tablet-friendly, but embedding is unknown.
- **Interactive (solving together):** NRICH, "Solving Together - Got it!" https://nrich.maths.org/14442 [V]. Includes a tablet version, a PDF and a video intro.
- **Printable:** NRICH Nim-7 (take 1 or 2 counters; taking the last one loses). https://nrich.maths.org/content/id/10524/game.html [V]. Rules only, played with physical counters.
- **Video:** MENACE: the pile of matchboxes which can learn. Stand-up Maths (Matt Parker). `R9c-_neaxeU` [V]. Length unconfirmed, roughly 15-20 min, so clip it. A matchbox "AI" learns noughts and crosses. Great hook for AI-curious kids.
- **Hook:** Nimatron, the 1940 World's Fair Nim-playing machine. JSTOR Daily: https://daily.jstor.org/the-nimatron and Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimatron
- **Not verified:** archimedes-lab.org Nim and cut-the-knot Nim pages could not be fetched (blocked). Avoid cut-the-knot, which historically used Java.

### B3. Pirate and lion puzzles
- **Top video:** Can you solve the pirate riddle? (Alex Gendler). TED-Ed. `Mc6VA7Q1vXQ` [V]. About 5 min.
  - Pirates splitting gold, with "thrown overboard" stakes. Cartoon, not graphic.
- **Video:** Can you solve the wizard standoff riddle? (Daniel Finkel). TED-Ed. `mmkCS5eA4f8` [V via ed.ted]. 5:26.
  - A three-way duel (a "truel") where wands turn people into fish or statues. Shows how the weakest player can win by "missing on purpose." Low violence concern.
- **Lions-and-sheep puzzle:** no vetted kid-safe video was found. The tutor can present it as a text puzzle (lions eat the sheep, mild).

### B4. First-mover advantage, commitment, burning bridges
- **Video:** Game Theory 101 (#22): Tying Hands and Burning Bridges. Spaniel. `bxIrKkGRpBY` [V]. Short. Ages 14+.
- **Video (second-mover advantage):** Can you solve the monster duel riddle? (Alex Gendler). TED-Ed. https://ed.ted.com/lessons/can-you-solve-the-monster-duel-riddle-alex-gendler. 5:36.
  - You pick a disk first, then your opponent picks from the remaining two. Likely non-transitive dice (unconfirmed), so moving first is a disadvantage.
  - The YouTube ID could not be extracted; get it from the ed.ted page.
- **Hook:** "Burn the boats" (Cortés, 1519). Flag that the history is partly legend, and that the conquest context needs care.

### B5. Credible vs. empty threats
- **Video:** Spaniel's subgame perfect equilibrium / punishment strategies lessons. https://gametheory101.com/courses/game-theory-101/subgame-perfect-equilibrium/ and https://gametheory101.com/courses/game-theory-101/punishment-strategies/ (IDs not extracted).
- **Activity (tutor-led):** a parent/teen "If you don't clean your room, no phone for a month" game tree. The learner prunes threats the parent would not actually carry out.

### B6. Ultimatum and bargaining games
- **Top hook video:** Two Monkeys Were Paid Unequally (excerpt from Frans de Waal's TED Talk). TED Blog Video. `meiU6TxysCg` [V]. About 2-3 min.
  - A capuchin gets a cucumber while its neighbor gets grapes, and it throws the cucumber back. Funny and kid-safe.
  - Also hosted by British Council LearnEnglish: https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/general/video-zone/two-monkeys-were-paid-unequally
- **Activity:** EconEdLink, "Behavioral Economics: With 'Fairness', Other Things Matter" (Grades 9-12, 60 min). https://econedlink.org/resources/behavioral-economics-lesson-five-other-things-matter/ [V]
  - An ultimatum game in two rounds, plus "Econ vs. Human" discussion, a case study and the monkey video as an extension. The free lesson page needs no account; the related webinar does.
  - Solo with a tutor: they play 3 rounds as proposer and 3 as responder over 10 imaginary coins. The tutor rejects lowball offers, then they discuss why.
- **Activity:** British Council Vietnam / Exscitec, game theory activity. https://www.britishcouncil.vn/sites/default/files/activity_-_game_theory_activity_instructions_0.pdf [V]
  - A Red/Blue team PD over 7 rounds, plus a "Card Deal" coalition-bargaining game.
  - Built for groups. Only the Card Deal could be simulated, with the tutor voicing 3 AI players. It is the weakest fit.

### B7. Wars of attrition
- **Background:** Dollar auction (Wikipedia). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollar_auction
- **Activity (tutor-led):** play a dollar auction for 100 imaginary points, with the learner and tutor taking turns raising bids. Then discuss sunk costs.
- **Hook:** "last to leave the circle wins" YouTube challenge videos and staring contests.
  - **Flag:** no specific challenge video was vetted. Avoid linking influencer videos without preview.
- No short, vetted kid-friendly war-of-attrition video was found. Vsauce2 has relevant videos, but no ID was confirmed.

### C4. Signaling

### C5. Auctions and the winner's curse
- **Video:** Spaniel's Second Price Auctions lesson. https://gametheory101.com/courses/game-theory-101/second-price-auction/ (ID not extracted)
- **Activity (tutor-led classic):** "Jar of coins" common-value auction. The tutor describes a jar (or shows a photo) and the learner bids against 4 AI bidders the tutor simulates. The winner usually overpays, which is the winner's curse.
- **Hooks:**
  - The 2020 Nobel Prize in Economics (Milgrom and Wilson, auction theory). https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2020/popular-information/ [NF]
  - eBay last-second bidding
  - Esports and NBA draft-style bidding
