Summary: Solve a two-round fight and see why the simple answers have no fighting at all.

Sittings: 1

## Learning targets

## Tutor guidance
Flow: bet, clip, rematch, done. Before the clip, Rocky offers a bet (learner picks A, B or C and locks it in; settle it after the clip): "In a two-round fight-or-quit game, do the simple equilibria involve any real fighting? A) Yes, long fights. B) No: one player fights and the other quits right away. C) Both always quit." Answer: B. Clip: https://youtu.be/wR8dqbc6mlQ?t=1444&end=2873 (Ben Polak, Open Yale Courses). By time: 24:04 a two-round version solved backward; the pure answers where one side quits at once. Rematch (Rocky only): prize 4, cost 1 a round. Pure answer: one fights, the other quits, no cost paid. Mixed answer: each fights with chance prize/(prize + cost) = 4/5 = 80%. Why does that make the other side indifferent? (Fighting: 20% win 4, 80% pay 1 and continue worth 0, so 0.8 - 0.8 = 0, same as quitting.) The learner explains it in their own words.
