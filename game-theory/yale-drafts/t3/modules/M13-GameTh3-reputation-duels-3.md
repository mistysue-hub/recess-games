Backward induction says A) every rival enters, B) nobody enters, or C) half enter." Answer: A. Clip: https://youtu.be/SE7kP7XZuV4?end=1518 (Ben Polak, Open Yale Courses). By time: 0:00 Selten's chain-store paradox; 20:56 how a tiny chance the owner is 'crazy' makes fighting early worth it, plus real reputations. This rewinds to the start of the lecture. Rematch (Rocky only): suppose there's a 1-in-100 chance the owner actually enjoys fighting. Why might even a sensible owner fight the first rival? (A fight makes later rivals think he might be the fighting type, so they stay out.) Why does backward induction alone say he never fights? (In the last town, fighting only costs him, and that unravels back to the first.) The learner explains it in their own words.
